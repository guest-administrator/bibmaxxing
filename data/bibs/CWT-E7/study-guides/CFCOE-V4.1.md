# CFCOE v4.1 — Cyber Force Concept of Operations and Employment

> **⚠ Reference summary, not a full study guide.** CFCOE is CUI and not publicly released. Closest public substitutes: JP 3-12 Chapter III + FCC-C10F Strategic Plan (on disk).

**Bib study scope:** Annexes E, F.
**Classification / Availability:** CUI — FLTCYBERCOM / TENTH Fleet distribution.

## What this document is {#what-it-is}

CFCOE (Cyber Force Concept of Operations and Employment) is FLTCYBERCOM's force-employment doctrine for the Navy's Cyber Mission Force (CMF). Where NWP 3-12 provides Navy cyber doctrine broadly, CFCOE is the operational CONOPS — how Navy CMF teams are **aligned, tasked, sourced, and employed** day-to-day under FLTCYBERCOM.

Version 4.1 reflects post-2018 updates aligning Navy CMF to the USCYBERCOM Cyber Mission Force Full Operational Capability (FOC) construct declared in May 2018.

## The Navy CMF build-out {#cmf-buildout}

Context for v4.1:

- **2013:** USCYBERCOM directed standup of **133 CMF teams** across services.
- **2014–2018:** CMF team buildout, phased Initial Operational Capability (IOC) to Full Operational Capability (FOC) milestones.
- **May 2018:** USCYBERCOM elevated to unified combatant command; CMF declared at FOC.
- **2019–present:** CMF rebalancing and CMF 2.0 discussions (modernizing team construct for evolving mission).

Navy's share of the 133 is **approximately 40 teams** (exact number not consistently public; reflects Navy's portion based on service CMF allocation). CFCOE describes how these teams are organized, developed, and employed.

## Navy CMF team types {#navy-cmf}

Same five CMF team types as joint (USCYBERCOM) doctrine:

| Team type | Primary mission | Typical pairing |
|---|---|---|
| **CPT (Cyber Protection Team)** | DCO-IDM defending priority missions, networks, key terrain | Deployed to specific missions, rotated |
| **CMT (Combat Mission Team)** | OCO in support of CCMD operational plans | Aligned to geographic CCMDs |
| **CST (Combat Support Team)** | Intel / analytic support to CMT | Co-deployed with CMTs |
| **NMT (National Mission Team)** | Strategic defense against top-tier nation-state threats | Under Cyber National Mission Force |
| **NST (National Support Team)** | Intel / analytic support to NMT | Co-deployed with NMTs |

Navy CMF teams are built from rated **CTs (Cryptologic Technician) sub-ratings** (CTN = networks; CTR = regulatory/CSN; CTT = technical; etc.), ITs (Information Systems Technician), IS (Intelligence Specialist), plus cyber warfare officers (1810s) and Information Warfare Officer community.

## Command relationships {#command}

CFCOE v4.1 codifies how Navy CMF teams flow authority:

### Administrative Control (ADCON)

ADCON = manning, training, equipping, organizing. Navy CMF teams are ADCON through:

- **NAVIFOR (Naval Information Forces)** — the TYCOM (Type Commander) for the Navy's Information Warfare Community.
- NAVIFOR handles manning pipeline, training standards, equipping, qualification.

### Operational Control (OPCON)

OPCON = mission execution authority. Navy CMF teams are OPCON through:

- **FLTCYBERCOM / C10F** — for Navy-level tasking and when Navy retains OPCON.
- **USCYBERCOM** — for CMF missions assigned to USCYBERCOM (most CMF work).
- Navy CMF teams on deployed CCMD missions may flow OPCON transfers — FLTCYBERCOM → USCYBERCOM → JFHQ-C → supported CCMD.

### Tactical Control (TACON)

TACON = tactical direction for specific mission. Typically held by the supported commander during the mission.

### Supporting / Supported relationships

For specific missions, FLTCYBERCOM may be a **supporting commander** to a geographic CCMD, providing Navy CMF capabilities to that CCMD's operational plan.

## Annexes E and F — expected scope {#annexes-e-f}

Most CONOPS documents follow a similar annex structure. For CFCOE, Annexes E and F likely cover:

- **Annex E: Team Employment / Task Alignment** — how Navy CMF teams (CPTs, CMTs, CSTs, NMTs, NSTs) are aligned to fleet commanders, CCMDs, and USCYBERCOM mission priorities. Which team types deploy, when, under whose authority.
- **Annex F: Command Relationships and Reporting** — USFLTCYBERCOM's OPCON/TACON relationships with subordinate Navy cyber units (NCDOC, Naval Information Operations Commands), plus reporting/support relationships with USCYBERCOM, CCMDs, and other services.

(Exact titles will differ; the Bib scope suggests these annexes are where force-employment and command-relationship questions will come from.)

## Force generation and sourcing {#sourcing}

CFCOE v4.1 covers how Navy CMF teams get generated:

1. **Manning pipeline** — NAVIFOR assigns rated CTs, ITs, IS, and officers into CMF billets.
2. **Individual training** — JCAC (Joint Cyberspace Analyst Course), service schools, on-the-job qualification.
3. **Team qualification** — team-level qualification exercises (Cyber Flag, Cyber Guard, service-level events).
4. **Continuous readiness** — C-rating / T-rating cycles, mission-essential task lists (METLs).
5. **Operational employment** — aligned to USCYBERCOM tasking or FLTCYBERCOM missions.

The Navy's enlisted cyber technical pipeline was historically the **CTN (Cryptologic Technician Networks)** rating. In June 2023 CTN was rebranded **CWT (Cyber Warfare Technician)** to align the rating name with its actual mission (defensive and offensive cyberspace operations) and with the broader cyber workforce naming outside the Navy. CWT E-7 reflects the senior enlisted leadership layer — Chiefs who lead CMF team elements or serve as senior watch/operations leaders.

## NAVIFOR and the IW community {#navifor}

**NAVIFOR (Naval Information Forces)** is the TYCOM for Navy's Information Warfare (IW) community:

- Located in Suffolk, VA.
- Parents CTs, ITs, IS, Aerographer's Mates, and Navy cyber officers.
- Owns training pipelines (CIWT — Center for Information Warfare Training).
- Provides TYCOM oversight to NIOCs administratively.

**IW community** includes: CWO (Cyber Warfare Officers, 1810), CW (Cyber Warrants, 781X), and enlisted ratings CT, IT, IS.

## What's publicly known about Navy CMF employment {#public-employment}

From public sources (JP 3-12, USCYBERCOM fact sheets, NSArchive FOIA releases):

- **Cyber Mission Force categories** (all services, including Navy):
  - **Cyber Protection Teams (CPTs)** — DCO
  - **Combat Mission Teams (CMTs)** — OCO in support of CCMD operations
  - **Combat Support Teams (CSTs)** — supporting CMTs with intelligence/targeting
  - **National Mission Teams (NMTs)** — defend the nation against strategic cyber threats
  - **National Support Teams (NSTs)** — support NMTs
- **FLTCYBERCOM's command relationships:**
  - ADCON — Navy (via NAVIFOR for manning/training/equipping)
  - OPCON — USCYBERCOM for cyber missions
  - Supporting commander — to fleet commanders for cyber effects
- **TENTH Fleet** is the numbered-fleet warfighting instrument under FLTCYBERCOM; the two are the same organization under two hats.

## Exam-testable concepts {#testable}

- What version of CFCOE is on the Bib? **v4.1**.
- Who publishes CFCOE? **USFLTCYBERCOM / TENTH Fleet**.
- Under what command authority does a Navy CPT deploy? **USCYBERCOM OPCON** when executing CMF missions; Navy ADCON for manning/training.
- What distinguishes a CMT from a CPT? **CMT executes OCO in support of CCMDs; CPT executes DCO defending priority missions.**
- Where does the Navy IO / Cyber enlisted workforce flow from? **NAVIFOR (TYCOM for ADCON) → FLTCYBERCOM (OPCON) → subordinate NCDOC / NIOCs**.
- How many CMF teams total were directed across services? **133**.
- When did CMF achieve FOC? **May 2018, with USCYBERCOM's elevation to unified combatant command.**
- Which Navy rating is the primary cyber technical enlisted rating? **CTN (Cryptologic Technician Networks).**
- What is NAVIFOR? **Naval Information Forces — Navy's Information Warfare TYCOM.**
- What training course qualifies CMF members? **JCAC (Joint Cyberspace Analyst Course).**
