# SECNAVINST 5510.30C — DON Personnel Security Program

**Bib study scope:** Chapter 1 (lives inside the former SECNAV M-5510.30 manual content, which 5510.30C subsumed).

**Source on disk:** `SECNAV-M-5510.30 DON Personnel Security Program Manual.pdf` (full 2006 manual — policies still define the program framework) and `SECNAVINST-5510.30C DON Personnel Security Program.pdf` (the 2020 instruction that superseded and replaced the manual).

## Why this reference matters {#why}

SECNAVINST 5510.30 and its predecessor manual govern **who in the Department of the Navy gets access to classified information, how that access is granted, who's responsible, and how it's maintained or revoked.** Every Chief supervising people with clearances is held to this instruction's standards. The exam will test:

- What level of clearance requires what type of investigation.
- Who has continuous-evaluation responsibilities.
- What triggers a mandatory reporting requirement.
- How denial, suspension, and revocation work.

## Executive Orders + the framework {#eo-framework}

**EO 12968 (Access to Classified Information)** is the baseline national-level authority. DoD implements it via **DoD Manual 5200.02** (public). The Navy implements that via **SECNAVINST 5510.30C** (and formerly SECNAV M-5510.30).

The hierarchy to memorize:

```
President (EO 12968, EO 13526)
     │
     ▼
DoD (DoDM 5200.02 Personnel Security Program)
     │
     ▼
Navy (SECNAVINST 5510.30C — the Navy implementation)
     │
     ▼
Command-level implementation (your command's local PSP)
```

**EO 13526** handles classification itself (Confidential/Secret/Top Secret); EO 12968 handles *personnel access* to it. Don't confuse them on the exam.

## Clearance levels and what they permit {#clearance-levels}

U.S. classification levels, from lowest to highest:

| Level | Damage if compromised | Investigation typically required |
|---|---|---|
| **Confidential** | Damage to national security | **Tier 3** (T3, formerly NACLC) |
| **Secret** | Serious damage | **Tier 3** (T3) |
| **Top Secret** | Exceptionally grave damage | **Tier 5** (T5, formerly SSBI) |
| **SCI** (compartmented) | Adjudicated separately by DNI channels | **Tier 5** + SCI eligibility via ICD 704 |

**Critical memorize:**
- "Confidential" = "damage" (no qualifier).
- "Secret" = "serious damage."
- "Top Secret" = "exceptionally grave damage."
- SCI is **not** a clearance level — it's a compartmented access category layered on top of a Top Secret clearance.

## The investigation tiers (post-2017 Federal Investigations Standards) {#tiers}

The old language (NACI, NACLC, SSBI) is being phased out in favor of tier numbers:

| New tier | Old name | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | NACI | Non-sensitive public trust |
| Tier 2 | MBI | Moderate risk public trust |
| Tier 3 | NACLC / ANACI | Secret and Confidential clearances |
| Tier 4 | BI | High-risk public trust (no clearance) |
| Tier 5 | SSBI | Top Secret and TS/SCI clearances |

**Reinvestigations:**
- **Tier 3R** — periodic reinvestigation for Secret / Confidential every 10 years historically.
- **Tier 5R** — reinvestigation for TS / TS-SCI every 5 years historically.
- Now transitioning to **Continuous Vetting (CV)** — replaces the periodic model with always-on checks of commercial data, court records, credit, etc.

## The adjudicative guidelines (the 13 factors) {#guidelines}

Clearance adjudication weighs 13 whole-person factors from the national adjudicative guidelines:

1. Allegiance to the United States
2. Foreign influence
3. Foreign preference
4. Sexual behavior
5. Personal conduct
6. Financial considerations
7. Alcohol consumption
8. Drug involvement and substance misuse
9. Psychological conditions
10. Criminal conduct
11. Handling protected information
12. Outside activities
13. Use of information technology

Each factor has specific disqualifying and mitigating conditions. A single disqualifier isn't automatic denial; the adjudicator weighs the **whole person**.

## Roles and responsibilities {#roles}

### Command level

- **Commanding Officer (CO)** — ultimate responsibility for the command's personnel security program.
- **Security Manager (SM)** — command's lead on personnel security. Usually E-7 and above, with appropriate training. Oversees investigation requests, access determinations, debriefings.
- **Special Security Officer (SSO)** — owns SCI access and the command SCIF if one exists. Separate from the Security Manager in most larger commands.
- **Security Assistant** — day-to-day execution.

### DoN level

- **DUSN(SP&I)** — Under Secretary of the Navy for Security & Intelligence. Principal advisor to SECNAV on personnel security.
- **DoN CAF** — Department of the Navy Central Adjudication Facility (legacy name). Adjudicates clearance eligibility determinations. Merged into **DoD CAF** operationally post-2012 but Navy still has equities.

## Access granting workflow {#workflow}

1. **Position sensitivity determined** — HR/command identifies the billet requires clearance.
2. **Investigation requested** — Security Manager submits SF-86 / eApp via DISS (Defense Information System for Security; successor to JPAS).
3. **Investigation conducted** — by DCSA (Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency).
4. **Adjudication** — DoD CAF (or legacy DoN CAF) reviews investigation against the 13 guidelines, makes eligibility determination.
5. **Access granted** — CO/Security Manager grants access based on need-to-know plus valid eligibility.
6. **Indoctrination / NDA** — member signs **SF-312 Classified Information Nondisclosure Agreement**.
7. **Continuous vetting + periodic reinvestigation** — ongoing.

## Reportable information (what subjects must self-report) {#reporting}

Clearance holders must report to their Security Manager:

- Foreign travel (personal, not official).
- Foreign contacts (close/continuing foreign national relationships).
- Arrests, charges, citations (any criminal involvement, even without conviction).
- Financial anomalies (bankruptcy, garnishments, significant debt).
- Changes in marital status / cohabitation.
- Mental-health treatment involving a threat-of-harm evaluation (specific exceptions in DoDM 5200.02 protect routine counseling).
- Drug use (any illegal; also certain prescription misuse).
- Adverse administrative actions (civil suits, loss of professional license).

**Failure to report is itself a Guideline E (personal conduct) issue.**

## Denial, suspension, revocation {#denial}

- **Suspension** — temporary halt of access pending review (e.g., a positive drug test, an arrest, foreign-travel disclosure under review).
- **Denial** — clearance never granted after adjudication.
- **Revocation** — previously granted clearance removed.

**Due process** — subject receives a **Statement of Reasons (SOR)** explaining the preliminary determination, has opportunity to respond, and may appeal. Navy's appeal process goes through DoD CAF and ultimately DOHA (Defense Office of Hearings and Appeals) for personnel.

## SF-86 — the application {#sf86}

The **Standard Form 86 (Questionnaire for National Security Positions)** is the authoritative disclosure form. Key sections:

- Personal info, citizenship, residence history.
- Education, employment history (typically 10 years).
- Foreign contacts, travel, financial interests.
- Criminal history (any-time).
- Mental health, drug/alcohol, financial.
- Military service, investigations.

**Filed electronically via eApp in DISS.** Subject must sign a release authorizing checks + a medical release where applicable.

## The Insider Threat Program (ITP) overlay {#itp}

SECNAVINST 5510.30C integrates with the DoN Insider Threat Program, which requires:

- Centralized analysis of user activity monitoring (UAM) across classified networks.
- Integration with personnel security (a cleared person's reported adverse info feeds ITP).
- Training for all cleared personnel annually (typically part of SAEDA / insider-threat refresher).

**SAEDA** — Subversion and Espionage Directed Against the Army — the foundational insider-threat training topic (adapted for Navy).

## Exam-testable concepts

- What is the "damage test" for TS? **Exceptionally grave damage.**
- What Tier investigation is required for TS access? **Tier 5 (T5, formerly SSBI).**
- What EO governs personnel access to classified? **EO 12968.**
- Who adjudicates DoN clearances? **DoD CAF** (successor to DoN CAF).
- What form is the classified NDA? **SF-312.**
- What's the command lead for personnel security? **Security Manager.**
- What agency does background investigations? **DCSA** (Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency).
- If a Sailor is arrested on leave, what must they do? **Self-report to the Security Manager as soon as possible.**
- What replaces periodic reinvestigations? **Continuous Vetting (CV).**
- Number of adjudicative guidelines? **13 (the whole-person factors).**
- What system do Security Managers use to request/manage clearances now? **DISS** (Defense Information System for Security), replacing JPAS.
