# JP 3-60 — Joint Targeting

**Bib study scope:** Chapters I, II; Executive Summary.

**Source on disk:** `JP-3-60 Joint Targeting.pdf`.

## Why this matters {#why}

Joint targeting is how the joint force **selects, prioritizes, and matches capabilities to targets** to create desired effects. This includes kinetic effects (missiles, air strikes) and non-kinetic (cyber, EW, MISO). Cyberspace effects are integrated through the joint targeting cycle — JP 3-12 Ch IV references JP 3-60.

## Targeting fundamentals (Chapter I) {#fundamentals}

### Definitions

- **Target** — an entity or object considered for possible engagement or other action.
- **Targeting** — the process of selecting and prioritizing targets and matching the appropriate response, considering operational requirements and capabilities.
- **Effect** — the physical or behavioral state of a system resulting from an action.
- **Engagement** — the action against a specific target.

### Target types

- **Deliberate targets** — identified and planned for in advance; on a target list.
- **Dynamic targets** — identified too late for deliberate planning; engaged via time-sensitive or priority procedures.
  - **Time-Sensitive Targets (TSTs)** — highest priority dynamic targets with narrow engagement windows.
  - **Targets of Opportunity** — targets that appear with chance for engagement.
  - **Emerging targets** — dynamic targets of potentially significant value.

### Effect types (the 5 Ds + more)

Targeting creates effects on targets:

- **Deny** — temporarily prevent use.
- **Degrade** — reduce effectiveness.
- **Disrupt** — temporarily interrupt.
- **Destroy** — permanently damage to uselessness.
- **Defeat** — render incapable of accomplishing mission.
- **Delay, Divert, Deceive, Disorient, Dissuade** — further specific effects.

Cyberspace operations typically produce **Deny, Degrade, Disrupt, Destroy** — the 4 Ds on the target's functionality rather than its physical form.

### Target lists

- **Joint Target List (JTL)** — all approved targets.
- **Joint Integrated Prioritized Target List (JIPTL)** — targets prioritized against the commander's objectives for a given period.
- **Restricted Target List (RTL)** — targets with restrictions on engagement (specific conditions required).
- **No-Strike List (NSL)** — targets explicitly not to be engaged (protected by law, policy, sensitivity).

## The Joint Targeting Cycle (Chapter II core) {#cycle}

**Six phases — memorize:**

1. **End State and Commander's Objectives**
2. **Target Development and Prioritization**
3. **Capabilities Analysis**
4. **Commander's Decision and Force Assignment**
5. **Mission Planning and Force Execution**
6. **Assessment**

### Phase 1 — End State and Commander's Objectives

Link targeting to the commander's desired end state. Without clear objectives, targeting becomes arbitrary.

### Phase 2 — Target Development and Prioritization

Define the targets that will produce the desired effects:

- **Target system analysis** — how does the target system work? Nodes, links, dependencies?
- **Target validation** — legal review (LOAC/IHL compliance), CDE (Collateral Damage Estimate).
- **Prioritization** — build the JIPTL based on impact and feasibility.

### Phase 3 — Capabilities Analysis

Match capabilities to targets. What weapon/cyber tool/effect creates the desired outcome on this target? Consider:

- **Weaponeering** — specific munitions + delivery methods for kinetic targets.
- **Cyber capability matching** — for cyberspace effects; ties to USCYBERCOM + service cyber components.
- **Effects-based approach** — the goal is the effect, not the action.

### Phase 4 — Commander's Decision and Force Assignment

Commander approves the plan; forces are tasked.

- **ATO (Air Tasking Order)** — daily orders to air/space forces.
- **Cyber Tasking Order (CTO)** — USCYBERCOM's equivalent.

### Phase 5 — Mission Planning and Force Execution

Detailed planning by executing units, then execution.

### Phase 6 — Assessment

Did the action produce the intended effect?

- **Combat Assessment (CA)** — overall assessment of effectiveness.
- **Battle Damage Assessment (BDA)** — physical damage, functional damage, target-system impact.
- **Munitions Effectiveness Assessment (MEA)** — did the weapon/capability perform as expected?
- **Reattack Recommendation** — whether to strike again.

Cyberspace BDA is harder than physical BDA — effects may be transient, partial, or silently reversed. Assessment often requires dedicated collection.

## Target categorization {#categorization}

### By function (typical list)

- **Leadership** — adversary command element.
- **Command & Control** — HQ, comms.
- **Forces** — military units.
- **Sustainment** — logistics, fuel, ammunition.
- **WMD / CBRN** — weapons of mass destruction.
- **Infrastructure** — power, transportation, industry.
- **Information** — networks, decision-support systems.

Cyberspace targets typically map to **C2** and **Information** categories (and enable effects in the others).

## Collateral Damage Estimation (CDE) {#cde}

Before every engagement, a **CDE** is required — a systematic estimate of:

- Civilian casualties.
- Civilian structure damage.
- Environmental damage.
- Unintended secondary effects.

For cyberspace: **Collateral Effects Estimate** considers propagation beyond intended target — can the effect spread to civilian systems, other countries, other DoD systems?

CDE thresholds drive **target approval authority**:

- **Low CDE** — lower-echelon approval.
- **High CDE** — higher-echelon approval, potentially SECDEF or POTUS.

## Law of Armed Conflict (LOAC) considerations {#loac}

Every target must satisfy LOAC:

- **Distinction** — can distinguish between combatants and civilians.
- **Proportionality** — expected collateral harm not excessive relative to anticipated military advantage.
- **Military necessity** — the target contributes to accomplishing the military mission.
- **Humanity** — not unnecessarily cruel.

Cyber targets add complications: attribution, propagation, dual-use infrastructure (a hospital's network may be a legitimate military C2 channel during combat).

## Rules of Engagement (ROE) {#roe}

ROE from **CJCSI 3121.01B SROE/SRUF** establish what forces can engage, when, how. Targeting must comply with current ROE.

## Integration with cyberspace {#cyber-integration}

Cyberspace effects are integrated through the same targeting cycle:

- Targets identified, validated, prioritized.
- Cyber capabilities matched (USCYBERCOM CMTs, service cyber components).
- Approval considers intel gain/loss, attribution risk, collateral effects.
- Execution via Cyber Tasking Order or equivalent.
- Assessment includes cyber-specific BDA.

USCC INST 3000.14A governs deconfliction between cyber targeting actions.

## Exam-testable concepts

- How many phases in the Joint Targeting Cycle? **6.**
- What's phase 1? **End State and Commander's Objectives.**
- What's phase 6? **Assessment.**
- What's a JIPTL? **Joint Integrated Prioritized Target List.**
- What's an NSL? **No-Strike List** — targets explicitly not to be engaged.
- What's an RTL? **Restricted Target List** — targets with engagement restrictions.
- The 4 Ds of effects? **Deny, Degrade, Disrupt, Destroy.**
- What's CDE? **Collateral Damage Estimate.**
- Four LOAC principles? **Distinction, Proportionality, Military Necessity, Humanity.**
- What's a TST? **Time-Sensitive Target** — highest-priority dynamic target with narrow engagement window.
- What document issues targeting to air/space forces? **ATO — Air Tasking Order.**
