# JP 3-13 — Information Operations

**Bib study scope:** Chapters II, IV.

**Source on disk:** `JP-3-13 Information Operations.pdf`.

## Why this matters {#why}

Information Operations (IO) is how the joint force **affects the decision-making of adversaries, others, and audiences** through information-related activities. As a CWT Chief, you're part of the IW community — your daily work directly contributes to joint IO effects. JP 3-13 is your home-field doctrine.

## The information environment (IE) {#ie}

**IE = physical + informational + cognitive dimensions:**

- **Physical dimension** — the infrastructure of information (networks, systems, platforms).
- **Informational dimension** — where information itself lives (content, data, messages).
- **Cognitive dimension** — where humans perceive, understand, decide, and act on information.

IO targets all three — especially the cognitive dimension. That's where decisions happen.

## Information-Related Capabilities (IRCs) — Chapter II core content {#ircs}

IRCs are **tools, techniques, or activities employed within a dimension of the information environment** to create effects and operationally desirable conditions.

### Five core IRCs

| IRC | Role |
|---|---|
| **Electronic Warfare (EW)** | Denial, degradation, deception through the electromagnetic spectrum |
| **Military Information Support Operations (MISO)** | Conveying selected information to foreign audiences to influence behavior (formerly PSYOP) |
| **Military Deception (MILDEC)** | Actions to deliberately mislead adversary military decision-makers |
| **Operations Security (OPSEC)** | Denying adversaries knowledge of friendly critical information |
| **Cyberspace Operations** | Effects in/through cyberspace (see JP 3-12) |

### Supporting IRCs

- **Public Affairs (PA)** — truthful communication with domestic and foreign audiences (note: PA and MISO are separate; PA content is always true for U.S. audiences).
- **Civil-Military Operations (CMO)** — interaction between military and civilian populations.
- **Defense Support to Public Diplomacy (DSPD)** — DoD activities supporting U.S. diplomatic goals.
- **Space Operations** — supporting IO through space-based sensors, comms, navigation.
- **Special Technical Operations (STO)** — classified capabilities.
- **Combat Camera (COMCAM)** — imagery of operations.
- **Intelligence** — supports IO with analysis.

## OPSEC — the IRC every Chief knows {#opsec}

Five-step OPSEC process (memorize):

1. **Identify critical information** — what's essential to mission success that adversary shouldn't know?
2. **Analyze threats** — who wants our CI, what can they do with it?
3. **Analyze vulnerabilities** — what friendly indicators might reveal CI?
4. **Assess risk** — combine probability + impact.
5. **Apply countermeasures** — reduce vulnerability or change behavior.

**EEFIs** — Essential Elements of Friendly Information — the negation of CI; what adversary wants to know. Reporting suspected compromises through the chain is a Chief's job.

## Military Deception (MILDEC) {#mildec}

MILDEC manipulates adversary decision-making by deliberately creating false signals that the adversary will perceive, analyze, and act on in ways favorable to us.

**MILDEC planning** considers:
- The **target of deception** — who we want to fool.
- The **desired perception** — what we want them to believe.
- The **story** — coherent narrative the adversary can piece together.
- The **means** — which IRCs and activities plant the signals.
- The **feedback mechanism** — how we know the deception is working.

Important: MILDEC and OPSEC are **allies**. OPSEC hides real information; MILDEC substitutes false information.

## Integration with joint operations — Chapter IV scope {#ch-iv}

### Integration into JPP

IO is planned alongside all other operations via the Joint Planning Process (see JP 5-0):

- **Mission Analysis** — identify IO-relevant tasks, CCIRs, critical info.
- **COA Development** — include IO options in every COA.
- **COA Analysis** — wargame adversary information responses and friendly counter-responses.
- **Plan Development** — Annex C, Appendix 3 (Information Operations) details the IO plan.

### The IO Cell

Every joint force staff has (or should have) an IO Cell — staff element that:

- Plans and coordinates IRC employment.
- Synchronizes IO with fires, movement/maneuver, and intelligence.
- Assesses effects on target audiences.
- Represents IO at battle-rhythm events.

### Synchronization with other functions

IO must deconflict with:

- **Fires** — to avoid compromising kinetic effects with premature IO tipoffs.
- **Intelligence** — to balance intel gain/loss with effects.
- **Public Affairs** — PA is truth-based for U.S. audiences; MISO is foreign-audience influence. Must be walled.

### Assessment

IO uses **MOP and MOE** (see JP 5-0) specific to effects on target audiences:

- **MOP** — did we deliver the message / execute the EW burst / spoof the emitter?
- **MOE** — did adversary behavior change? (Harder to measure; often requires dedicated collection + analysis.)

## The relationship with cyberspace ops {#cyber-io}

Cyberspace operations are **both an IRC** (under JP 3-13) **and a full mission area** (under JP 3-12). The two publications are partners:

- JP 3-12 governs cyberspace operations regardless of purpose.
- JP 3-13 frames cyberspace operations when used to create IO effects.

A cyberspace operation might serve a purely technical goal (e.g., disrupt adversary C2 to support fires) or a purely informational goal (e.g., alter adversary leadership's perception of ground-truth via data manipulation). Either way, it's governed jointly.

## Modern doctrine: Information Advantage {#info-advantage}

JP 3-13 was formally rescinded and subsumed under JP 3-04 (Information in Joint Operations, 2022) — though the Bib scope still points to JP 3-13 because the CWT exam cycle tracks published doctrine on a lag. The newer doctrine reframes IO around **Information Advantage**:

- **Information Advantage** — the relative condition where a force has superior information and uses it better than the adversary.
- Aligns with the **Information Joint Function** added to JP 3-0 in 2017.
- Reorganizes IRCs into three broad activity categories: **understanding**, **defending information and information systems**, and **affecting the information environment**.

A CWT E-7 should know the classic five-IRC framework (exam-tested) and be aware that the modern framework emphasizes Information Advantage as the unifying concept.

## Target audience analysis {#audience}

IO is ineffective without understanding the **target audience (TA)**:

- **Decision-makers** — adversary commanders, political leaders whose decisions we aim to influence.
- **Influencers** — those close to decision-makers (staff, advisors, family, clergy).
- **Populations** — local populations whose sentiment affects operations.
- **Friendly forces** — domestic audiences (PA) and allied forces (PA + information sharing).

Target audience analysis (TAA) produces:
- **Demographics** — who they are.
- **Psychographics** — what they believe and value.
- **Media habits** — how they consume information.
- **Behavioral indicators** — what signals show response.
- **Vulnerabilities** — what concerns, fears, aspirations we can leverage.

## The fog of the information environment {#info-fog}

IO faces unique challenges:

- **Adversary information operations** — they're doing IO too, often in our networks and on our populations.
- **Attribution difficulty** — cyber-enabled IO is hard to attribute authoritatively.
- **Speed of narrative** — social media accelerates narrative beyond traditional staff cycles.
- **Deepfakes / synthetic media** — fabricated content blurs truth.
- **Domestic dimension restrictions** — Smith-Mundt limits what DoD can do domestically.
- **Allied coordination** — multinational IO requires painful consensus on message.

## The public affairs / MISO boundary {#pa-miso}

Critical doctrinal distinction:

| | Public Affairs | MISO |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | Domestic + foreign media/public | Foreign target audience |
| Truth | Always truthful | Based on truth but may emphasize selectively |
| Attribution | Always attributed to U.S. / DoD | May be un- or mis-attributed |
| Purpose | Inform, educate, build credibility | Influence behavior |

They must be separated organizationally and legally. U.S. law (Smith-Mundt and related) restricts MISO from targeting U.S. audiences.

## Exam-testable concepts

- Define IO. **Integrated employment during military operations of IRCs in concert with other LOOs to influence, disrupt, corrupt, or usurp adversary decision-making while protecting our own.**
- Name the five core IRCs. **EW, MISO, MILDEC, OPSEC, Cyberspace Operations.**
- What's the 2010 name change from PSYOP? **MISO (Military Information Support Operations).**
- Five steps of OPSEC? **Identify CI → Analyze threats → Analyze vulnerabilities → Assess risk → Apply countermeasures.**
- PA vs MISO — which is always truth-based for U.S. audiences? **PA.**
- What annex of an OPLAN holds the IO plan? **Annex C (Operations), Appendix 3.**
- What's the IO Cell? **Joint force staff element that plans/coordinates IRC employment and synchronizes IO.**
- EEFIs are? **Essential Elements of Friendly Information — what adversary wants to know.**
- What's the 2022 JP that subsumed JP 3-13? **JP 3-04 (Information in Joint Operations).**
- What replaced the "IRC" framing in modern JP 3-04 doctrine? **Information Advantage — the unifying concept.**
- What law restricts DoD from targeting U.S. audiences with MISO? **Smith-Mundt Act (and successor statutes/policy).**
- Three dimensions of the IE? **Physical, Informational, Cognitive.**
