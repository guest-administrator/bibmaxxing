# JP 3-09 — Joint Fire Support

**Bib study scope:** Chapter 3; Glossary.

**Source on disk:** `JP-3-09 Joint Fire Support.pdf`.

## Why this matters {#why}

Joint Fire Support coordinates **fires from multiple components** (air, ground, naval surface, cyber) to support ground maneuver. As a CWT Chief, you're part of the **non-kinetic fires** story — cyber effects integrated with kinetic ones.

Joint Fire Support is the joint-doctrine interface between **what targets to hit** (JP 3-60 Joint Targeting) and **how to hit them** (JP 3-09 Joint Fire Support). A cyber effect is, doctrinally, a form of fire support: it produces an effect on a target in coordination with other fires to support maneuver. Understanding how cyber plugs into the joint fires framework is the exam's expectation of a senior IW Sailor.

## Key terminology (from the Glossary) {#glossary}

### Fires vs fire support

- **Fires** — the use of weapon systems or other actions to create specific lethal or nonlethal effects on a target.
- **Fire support** — fires that directly support land, maritime, amphibious, and SOF forces to engage enemy forces, combat formations, and facilities in pursuit of tactical and operational objectives.
- **Joint fires** — fires produced during the employment of forces from two or more components in coordinated action toward a common objective.
- **Joint fire support** — joint fires that assist air, land, maritime, and special operations forces to move, maneuver, and control territory.

### Kinetic vs non-kinetic

- **Kinetic** — physical force (munitions, ballistics).
- **Non-kinetic** — non-physical effects (EW, cyber, MISO, deception).

Modern doctrine treats both under the same "fires" umbrella.

| Acronym | Full | Role |

## Chapter 3 scope — planning + execution {#ch-3}

### Fire support planning

Fires are planned through the Joint Planning Process (see JP 5-0):

1. **Mission Analysis** — understand what fires are required.
2. **COA Development** — fires as an element of each COA.
3. **Fires Support Plan** — integrates fires into the operation.

**Fire Support Coordinator (FSCOORD)** — senior officer responsible for fire support at echelon. Navy usually has a **Supporting Arms Coordination Center (SACC)** during amphibious operations, or a **Combined Operations Center (COC)** or **Tactical Air Control Squadron (TACRON)** depending on phase.

Planning considerations:

- **Target priority** — high-value targets receive first allocation of capable assets.
- **Asset availability** — which shooters can range the target within the required window.
- **Target effect required** — destruction, neutralization, suppression, or specific non-kinetic effect.
- **Collateral damage** — CDE per JP 3-60.
- **Coordination constraints** — FSCMs, airspace, ROE, PID requirements.
- **Weaponeering** — matching munitions (or cyber capability) to desired effect.

### Execution

- **Fires Cell** / **Joint Fires Element** — staff entity that integrates fires across components.
- **Air Operations Directive (AOD) + Air Tasking Order (ATO)** — drives air-delivered fires.
- **Naval Gunfire Support (NGFS) planning** — ship-delivered fires.
- **Cyber Tasking Order (CTO)** — non-kinetic cyber fires.
- **MDMP / JOPP synchronization** — fires synchronized with maneuver across the operation phase.

### The Air Tasking Order cycle (72-hour cycle)

The ATO drives air-delivered fires on a rolling 72-hour planning cycle:

- **ATO Day -3:** guidance, apportionment, Air Operations Directive published.
- **ATO Day -2:** target development, mission prioritization, Master Air Attack Plan (MAAP) develops.
- **ATO Day -1:** ATO/ACO/SPINS published; mission planning by crews.
- **ATO Day 0 (execution):** flight ops; dynamic targeting adjusts as needed.

Cyber effects requested against targets on the ATO are integrated through the joint fires cell and synchronized with the ATO cycle.

### Fire Support Coordination Measures (FSCMs)

Graphical controls on the battlefield to prevent friendly fire and to separate responsibilities:

- **Fire Support Coordination Line (FSCL)** — permissive measure; on the friendly side of FSCL, fires require coordination with ground maneuver commander; beyond it, fires are expedited (but still deconflicted).
- **Coordinated Fire Line (CFL)** — beyond which surface-to-surface fires are authorized without additional coordination.
- **Restricted Fire Area (RFA)** / **Restricted Fire Line (RFL)** — fires need specific permissions.
- **No-Fire Area (NFA)** — absolutely no fires except self-defense or specific clearance.
- **Free-Fire Area (FFA)** — fires authorized without additional coordination.
- **Airspace Coordination Area (ACA)** — coordinates aircraft with surface fires.

FSCMs are classified as **permissive** (FSCL, CFL, FFA — expedite fires) or **restrictive** (NFA, RFA, RFL — constrain fires).

### Cyber effects as joint fires

JP 3-09 + JP 3-12 integrate cyber into joint fires. Cyberspace effects can:

- **Suppress** adversary C2 to support SEAD/DEAD-equivalent cyber actions.
- **Disrupt** adversary fires at source (deny targeting data).
- **Degrade** adversary situational awareness.

These are **non-kinetic fires** — coordinated through the same targeting cycle (JP 3-60) and fires cell.

### The 4 Ds and the Joint Targeting cycle's effect verbs

Cyber effects doctrine shares vocabulary with fires doctrine. The **4 Ds**:

- **Deny** — withhold information, services, resources from the adversary.
- **Degrade** — reduce the effectiveness or efficiency of adversary capability.
- **Disrupt** — break or interrupt the adversary's flow of communication or operations.
- **Destroy** — permanently eliminate.

Fires doctrine adds:

- **Neutralize** — render the target ineffective for a period (reversibility implied).
- **Suppress** — temporarily reduce effectiveness (very transient).

A cyber effect might neutralize an adversary's targeting radar for the duration of a kinetic strike; an air strike might destroy the radar permanently. Both are fires; both integrate in the joint fires cell.

## Targeting interface {#targeting}

Joint Fire Support is the execution leg of Joint Targeting (JP 3-60):

```
JP 3-60 Targeting Cycle
        |
        v
   [JIPTL approved]
        |
        v
   [Targets matched to capabilities]
        |
        v
JP 3-09 Joint Fire Support — execution
```

The joint fires cell takes an approved target from the Joint Integrated Prioritized Target List (JIPTL), matches it with an appropriate asset (kinetic or non-kinetic), and tasks execution through the applicable tasking mechanism (ATO, NGFS plan, CTO, ground fires tasking).

## Navy-specific fire support {#navy-fires}

### Naval Surface Fire Support (NSFS)

- **Gunfire** from cruisers and destroyers: historically 5"/54 and 5"/62 guns (MK 45 series); limited range (~13-13.5 nm effective).
- **Missile fires** from Vertical Launch System (VLS) — Tomahawks for deep strike.
- **Ship-delivered SEAD** — via ship-based EW / electronic attack when applicable.
- **Amphibious fires** — supporting landing forces during amphibious ops.

### Cyber fires from afloat

The Carrier Strike Group / Expeditionary Strike Group IWC can request cyber effects in support of the maneuver commander. The requests flow through FLTCYBERCOM → USCYBERCOM as detailed in NWP 3-12 and OPTASK Cyberspace Operations.

### Fleet targeting coordination

- **Combined Operations Center (COC)** — strike group fires coordination hub.
- **Supporting Arms Coordination Center (SACC)** — specific to amphibious operations.
- **Maritime Headquarters (MHQ) / MOC** — theater-level Navy fires coordination.

## Exam-testable concepts

- What's SEAD? **Suppression of Enemy Air Defenses.**
- Difference between SEAD and DEAD? **SEAD temporarily neutralizes; DEAD permanently destroys.**
- What's CAS? **Close Air Support** — air fires in proximity to friendly troops.
- What's NSFS? **Naval Surface Fire Support.**
- What does FSCL mean and why does it matter? **Fire Support Coordination Line — on friendly side requires coordination, beyond it fires are expedited. Prevents fratricide.**
- What's an NFA? **No-Fire Area** — no fires inside.
- What's a JTAC? **Joint Terminal Attack Controller** — controls CAS engagements.
- Who's the FSCOORD? **Fire Support Coordinator — senior officer for fire support at echelon.**
- Document that drives daily air-delivered fires? **Air Tasking Order (ATO).**
- Can cyber effects be "fires"? **Yes — non-kinetic fires integrate with kinetic through the joint fires cell and targeting cycle.**
- What's the ATO planning cycle length? **72 hours** (rolling 72-hour cycle).
- What FSCMs are permissive vs restrictive? **Permissive: FSCL, CFL, FFA. Restrictive: NFA, RFA, RFL.**
- Where does a cyber fires request originate on a Navy strike group? **IWC (Information Warfare Commander)** — escalated through CWC and fleet to FLTCYBERCOM.
- What document tasks non-kinetic cyber fires day-to-day? **Cyber Tasking Order (CTO)**, analogous to ATO for air.
- What are the 4 Ds of effects? **Deny, Degrade, Disrupt, Destroy.**
