## What CRITIC is (publicly known) {#what-critic-is}

**CRITIC** stands for **Critical Information** — the highest-priority precedence category for intelligence reporting. CRITIC messages are reports of information whose timely delivery to the President or other senior national officials is of such importance that it must take precedence over all other traffic.

The term, concept, and existence of the CRITIC reporting system are unclassified. The specific handbook — with its procedures, triggering criteria, and reporting formats — is classified.

## Message precedence and where CRITIC sits {#message-precedence}

U.S. military messaging uses a standard precedence hierarchy (from lowest to highest):

| Precedence | Abbreviation | Intended delivery | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Routine** | R | 6 hours | Admin, non-urgent |
| **Priority** | P | 3 hours | Priority ops, routine ops reports |
| **Immediate** | O | 30 minutes | Crisis, time-sensitive ops |
| **Flash** | Z | As fast as possible | Enemy contact, major tactical events |
| **Flash Override** | W | Immediate override | National-level emergencies |
| **CRITIC** | CRITIC | Presidential delivery in ~10 minutes | Critical intelligence of national-level consequence |

CRITIC is its own precedence beyond FLASH / FLASH OVERRIDE for intelligence reporting. The ~10-minute publicly-associated delivery target is a historical norm; actual procedures are classified.

## Public framework around CRITIC {#public-framework}

- **Originator:** typically NSA / SIGINT elements, but CRITIC authority exists in other parts of the IC.
- **Criteria:** information that meets specific triggering thresholds related to imminent threats to national security, major hostile actions, or similar high-consequence events.
- **Precedence:** CRITIC takes precedence over FLASH, IMMEDIATE, PRIORITY, and ROUTINE message precedences in U.S. military communications.
- **Reporting target:** President and National Command Authority within a specific short timeline after origin (publicly associated historically with ~10 minutes).

## Historical examples of CRITIC-class reporting (public) {#historical}

Public references to CRITIC reporting have appeared in declassified materials:

- **Cuban Missile Crisis (1962)** — SIGINT-sourced CRITIC-category reporting informed the initial national response.
- **9/11 response** — internal after-action reporting referenced CRITIC channels in the hours following attacks.
- **ICBM launch detection** — missile early warning systems feed CRITIC-class reporting when thresholds are met.

These historical references demonstrate the category of event that triggers CRITIC — strategic events with presidential-decision consequences.

## CRITIC release authority (public concept) {#release-authority}

Because CRITIC reports are consequential, the **release authority** is typically held at specific designated levels — senior watch officers at qualifying nodes, command duty officers at specific commands, and the DIRNSA or equivalent for NSA-originated CRITICs.

- Release authority is not delegated casually.
- The authorized releaser bears personal responsibility for the correctness and necessity of the CRITIC.
- False or misfiled CRITICs have consequences — both for the individual and for the credibility of the reporting channel.

## CRITIC vs tactical urgent reporting {#critic-vs-tactical}

Watch the distinction:

- **CRITIC** = national-level strategic intelligence requiring presidential awareness.
- **FLASH precedence** = tactical urgency — enemy contact, major tactical events, time-sensitive operational reports.
- **OPREP-3 Navy Blue / PINNACLE** = Navy-specific urgent operational reports going to senior Navy leadership.

CRITIC is not "very fast message." It's a distinct category with specific substantive thresholds — the content must meet national-level threshold, not just be urgent.

## USSID 18 still applies {#ussid-18}

A CRITIC that touches U.S. person information must still comply with USSID 18 minimization. The urgency of CRITIC does not waive USSID 18 — in fact, the high visibility of CRITIC reporting means USSID 18 compliance is especially scrutinized.

## Exam-testable concepts (from public knowledge) {#testable}

- What does CRITIC stand for? **Critical Information** (reporting category).
- What agency primarily originates CRITIC reports? **NSA / SIGINT elements** (though other IC agencies can originate).
- What is the delivery target of a CRITIC? **Senior national leadership including the President.**
- Where does CRITIC rank in message precedence? **Highest — above FLASH and FLASH OVERRIDE for intelligence reporting.**
- What is the publicly-associated CRITIC delivery time target? **Approximately 10 minutes.**
- Does USSID 18 apply to CRITIC content? **Yes — minimization rules apply regardless of precedence.**
- Who holds release authority for CRITIC messages? **Specific designated senior watch / command positions; not casually delegated.**
- Is CRITIC a tactical-urgency precedence? **No — it is a strategic-consequence category, not just speed.**
