# NWP 3-12 — Navy Cyberspace Operations (Feb 2018 Ed)

> **⚠ Reference summary, not a full study guide.** The Navy edition of this publication is CUI/FOUO and not publicly released. This page summarizes the scope and points you at the public material (JP 3-12, AFDP 3-12, NATO AJP-3.20, Army War College guide) that carries substantively the same content.

**Bib study scope:** Chapter 2; Annexes A, B.
**Classification / Availability:** CUI / Controlled — Navy internal distribution (typically accessible on NIPR SharePoint, MyNavy Portal, or via your command's training SharePoint).

## What this document is {#what-it-is}

NWP 3-12 is the **Navy Warfare Publication** implementing Joint Publication 3-12 (Joint Cyberspace Operations) for U.S. Navy forces. It was issued in February 2018 as a companion to JP 3-12's June 2018 release. NWP 3-12 translates joint doctrine into Navy-specific command relationships, Fleet Cyber Command / TENTH Fleet authorities, and fleet-level procedures.

Think of the doctrinal stack this way:

- **JP 3-12** sets joint doctrine for every service.
- **NWP 3-12** re-expresses JP 3-12 for Navy forces, wiring joint mission areas into Navy command relationships.
- **OPTASK Cyberspace Operations** is the operational tasking message that directs Navy units how to execute NWP 3-12 today.
- **CFCOE-V4.1 / FDCD-2017** are the Navy-specific CONOPs layered on top, at the Fleet Cyber level.

The exam can sample from any rung of this stack, but questions explicitly labeled "Navy cyberspace doctrine" almost always key to NWP 3-12 (and, by extension, JP 3-12).

## Why the Bib tests it and NOT JP 3-12 verbatim {#navy-specific-scope}

The Navy exam leans on Navy-specific doctrine for a reason: your advancement exam measures how a Sailor would execute cyberspace operations inside the Navy's command structure, not just recite joint framework. NWP 3-12 specifies:

- **FLTCYBERCOM / TENTH Fleet as Navy's USCYBERCOM service component.** Tenth Fleet is the numbered-fleet instrument; FLTCYBERCOM is the command echelon.
- **NCDOC (Navy Cyber Defense Operations Command)** role in Navy DODIN operations and DCO.
- **Navy Cyber Mission Force team composition and alignment** — Navy CMTs, CSTs, CPTs, NSTs aligned to Fleet commanders, CCMDs, and USCYBERCOM.
- **OPNAV N2/N6 and NAVIFOR TYCOM roles** in manning, training, and equipping Navy cyber forces.

## Fleet Cyber Command / TENTH Fleet — background {#fltcyber-c10f}

USFLTCYBERCOM was established **29 January 2010** at Fort Meade, MD, concurrent with the reactivation of **U.S. Tenth Fleet** (a WWII-era ASW numbered fleet, reestablished as the operational arm of FLTCYBERCOM). The arrangement parallels other service cyber components:

- **Army:** Army Cyber Command (ARCYBER) / 2nd Army (the operational numbered formation).
- **Air Force:** 16th Air Force (AFCYBER) — merged 24 AF (cyber) and 25 AF (ISR) in 2019.
- **Marine Corps:** MARFORCYBER.
- **Navy:** FLTCYBERCOM / C10F.
- **Space Force:** Space Operations Command — cyber contribution still developing.

Each SCC provides its service-sourced **Cyber Mission Force** teams to USCYBERCOM and operates its service's portion of the DODIN. For the Navy, FLTCYBERCOM is **four-star reporting**: the Commander, FLTCYBERCOM is a Vice Admiral reporting operationally to USCYBERCOM and service-administratively through the CNO. FLTCYBERCOM's subordinate commands include:

- **NCDOC** (Navy Cyber Defense Operations Command, Suffolk VA) — Navy-wide DODIN monitoring / DCO-IDM.
- **NIOCs** (Navy Information Operations Commands) — located at Sugar Grove, Norfolk, Pensacola, Georgia, Hawaii, Texas, Whidbey Island, Yokosuka, etc. Each NIOC is a subordinate command executing SIGINT, CNE, or Navy CMF missions depending on the site.
- **NAVCYBERFOR** (Navy Cyber Forces, renamed/restructured over time; currently aligned under NAVIFOR TYCOM for administrative control).
- **Navy CMF teams** — Navy-sourced personnel filling CPTs, CMTs, CSTs, NMTs, NSTs aligned per USCYBERCOM tasking.

## Chapter 2 scope — what to study {#chapter-2}

Chapter 2 of NWP 3-12 mirrors JP 3-12 Chapter II (the three cyberspace mission areas) but re-frames them for Navy execution:

### DODIN Operations (Navy slice) {#dodin-ops-navy}

Navy owns and operates a large, highly-segmented slice of the DODIN:

- **NMCI (Navy Marine Corps Intranet)** — the ashore unclassified/secret enterprise network serving CONUS and many OCONUS Navy/USMC shore commands. Operated under a long-running services contract; Navy manages requirements and governance.
- **ONE-Net** — OCONUS equivalent for non-NMCI regions.
- **CANES (Consolidated Afloat Networks and Enterprise Services)** — the underway-ship network platform replacing ISNS, SCI Networks afloat, and others. Carries SIPR, JWICS, and SCI enclaves at sea.
- **Submarine networks** — SubLAN and specialized submarine comms.
- **Tactical/deployed networks** — SEAL/NSW, expeditionary units, tactical joint terminals.

DODIN Operations on these networks is a joint responsibility: **DISA** provides backbone services, **Navy CIO/NAVWAR (formerly SPAWAR)** provides many of the engineering authorities, **NCDOC** monitors and responds, and the **supported operational commander** retains tactical control.

### Defensive Cyberspace Operations (Navy DCO) {#dco-navy}

- **DCO-IDM** on Navy networks is primarily executed by **NCDOC** with CPT augmentation when a specific threat warrants.
- **DCO-RA** (response actions outside friendly cyberspace) is **not a Navy organic authority**. It flows through USCYBERCOM to SECDEF/POTUS and is directed back to the executing force (often a CMT, not a CPT).
- Navy CPTs deploy to protect priority missions — carrier strike groups, amphibious ready groups, forward-deployed shore nodes, specific platforms or weapons systems during key phases.
- DCO authorities for Navy action within Navy networks sit with FLTCYBERCOM; escalation to USCYBERCOM is required when effects may reach beyond the Navy boundary.

### Offensive Cyberspace Operations (Navy OCO contribution) {#oco-navy}

- Navy CMTs and CSTs execute OCO **only** under USCYBERCOM direction. Navy does not self-direct OCO.
- Approvals for OCO typically require SECDEF or POTUS authority (per JP 3-12 Chapter III and JP 3-60 approval tiers).
- A Navy CMT "aligned to" a combatant command executes that CCMD's cyberspace tasks as directed through USCYBERCOM.
- Navy's institutional knowledge in maritime targeting, submarine/ISR access, and forward-deployed presence feeds OCO mission planning even when the firing platform is not Navy.

## Annexes A and B — likely content {#annexes}

- **Annex A** typically covers **Navy Cyber Mission Force team structure and task alignment** — how each team type supports which commander. Key terms: "aligned to," "combat mission team (CMT)," "combat support team (CST)."
- **Annex B** usually covers **integration with joint operations and service cyber components** — how Navy CMF fits under USCYBERCOM's JFHQ-Cyber structure.

Specific team types (common to all services, per JP 3-12 and CPT-ORG-3-33.4):

| Team | Primary mission | Supported commander |
|---|---|---|
| **CPT** (Cyber Protection) | DCO — defend priority DoD missions | USCYBERCOM, CCMDs, services |
| **CMT** (Combat Mission) | OCO — support CCMD objectives | Geographic CCMD through USCYBERCOM |
| **CST** (Combat Support) | Intel + planning support to CMTs | Paired CMT |
| **NMT** (National Mission) | Defend nation from strategic cyber threats | USCYBERCOM directly |
| **NST** (National Support) | Intel + planning support to NMTs | Paired NMT |

Navy provides personnel to all five team types. The exam often tests whether a team is defensive (CPT) or offensive (CMT/NMT), and whether it operates inside (CPT, DCO-IDM) or outside (CMT, NMT, DCO-RA) friendly cyberspace.

## Navy Information Warfare (IW) community structure {#iw-structure}

NWP 3-12 operates inside the Navy's Information Warfare Community — the cluster of ratings and officer designators who staff Navy cyber operations:

- **Enlisted ratings:** CWT (Cyber Warfare Technician — the primary cyber enlisted rating; rebranded from CTN Cryptologic Technician Networks in June 2023), CTI (Interpretive — linguists), CTR (Collection), CTT (Technical), CTM (Maintenance), IT (Information Systems Technician), IS (Intelligence Specialist). CWT absorbed the former CTN billet stream; references to CTN in pre-2023 doctrine map to today's CWT.
- **Officer designators:** 1810 (Cryptologic Warfare Officer), 1820 (Information Professional), 1830 (Intelligence), 1840 (Cyber Warfare Engineer), 6440 (Information Warfare LDO), 7440 (Information Warfare CWO), plus 644X / 744X variants.
- **TYCOM:** **NAVIFOR (Naval Information Forces)**, headquartered Suffolk VA, is the Type Commander — manning/training/equipping authority for the IW community.
- **OPNAV code:** **N2/N6** is the OPNAV directorate for Information Warfare and the designated Navy lead for cyber requirements; current DCNO for Information Warfare holds a three-star billet.

TYCOM vs CCMD relationships are a frequent exam trap: **NAVIFOR trains and mans** the Sailor; **FLTCYBERCOM/C10F and subordinate NIOCs operationally control** the Sailor in a cyber billet; **USCYBERCOM has global OPCON** for CMF employment.

## Command relationships (COCOM / OPCON / TACON / ADCON) {#command-relationships}

Navy cyberspace command relationships follow the standard joint construct:

- **COCOM (Combatant Command authority)** — USCYBERCOM over CMF teams assigned to it; USINDOPACOM, USEUCOM, etc., over their assigned forces.
- **OPCON (Operational Control)** — USCYBERCOM delegates OPCON of specific teams to subordinate JFHQs (e.g., JFHQ-Cyber) or via supporting/supported relationships.
- **TACON (Tactical Control)** — typically exercised at the JFHQ / task-force level for specific mission windows.
- **ADCON (Administrative Control)** — through the Navy chain (FLTCYBERCOM → NAVIFOR TYCOM → the Sailor's command) for training, manning, discipline, fitness reports.

A Navy CMF Sailor has a TYCOM (NAVIFOR) for ADCON and an operational commander (FLTCYBERCOM/USCYBERCOM) for OPCON. This split is often tested: "Who writes your FITREP?" is ADCON; "who directs your mission?" is OPCON.

## Exam-testable concepts (from Navy-specific scope)

- Which command is Navy's USCYBERCOM service component? **USFLTCYBERCOM / TENTH Fleet**.
- Which Navy entity executes DCO-IDM on Navy networks? **NCDOC**.
- What is the TYCOM for Navy information warfare personnel? **NAVIFOR**.
- Which OPNAV code owns Navy cyber requirements? **N2/N6** (Information Warfare Directorate).
- Under what authority is Navy OCO conducted? **USCYBERCOM tasking with SECDEF/POTUS approval** — Navy does not self-direct OCO.
- Name a Navy CMF team type that operates outside DoD-owned cyberspace. **CMT (Combat Mission Team) executing OCO or NMT (National Mission Team) under USCYBERCOM direction.**
- What Navy network platform is used afloat? **CANES** (Consolidated Afloat Networks and Enterprise Services).
- What Navy network serves CONUS shore commands? **NMCI** (Navy Marine Corps Intranet); **ONE-Net** OCONUS.
- Under what authority does a Navy CPT operate in Navy-owned cyberspace? **DCO-IDM — FLTCYBERCOM / subordinate NIOC direction.**
- Who provides ADCON to a Navy CTN Sailor serving on a CMF team? **NAVIFOR TYCOM** (through the Sailor's parent command).
- Who provides OPCON to that same Sailor during mission execution? **USCYBERCOM** (usually via JFHQ-Cyber and paired CCMD).
- When was FLTCYBERCOM / TENTH Fleet established? **29 January 2010**.
