# Fleet Design for Cyber Defense (11 Aug 2017)

> **⚠ Reference summary.** The original FDCD document is restricted. Public adjacent material on disk: **FCC-C10F Strategic Plan 2015-2020** covers the same Tenth Fleet C2 scope.

**Bib study scope:** Key terms and definitions; Tenth Fleet C2.
**Classification / Availability:** CUI — FLTCYBERCOM / NAVIFOR distribution.

## What this document is {#what-it-is}

The Fleet Design for Cyber Defense (FDCD), dated 11 August 2017, is FLTCYBERCOM's strategic blueprint for how the Navy defends its networks and afloat forces against cyber threats. It was published during the Navy's major 2015–2018 push to mature cyber defense capabilities and shape the transition to the FY18 Cyber Mission Force Full Operational Capability.

The FDCD translates strategic guidance (NDS, Maritime Strategy, CNO Design, FLTCYBERCOM strategic plan) into a concrete design for Navy cyber defense: how forces are organized, where capabilities live, how C2 flows, and how the cyber defense mission is executed end-to-end.

## Why 2017 specifically {#context-2017}

The 2017 publication came at a pivot point for Navy cyber:

- **Cyber Mission Force (CMF)** was nearing Full Operational Capability (FOC) — originally targeted FY18.
- **Elevation of USCYBERCOM** to a unified combatant command was pending (occurred May 2018).
- **Fleet afloat networks** were transitioning to CANES from legacy ISNS, affecting how afloat cyber defense was architected.
- **NAVWAR (formerly SPAWAR)** was realigning with Project Overmatch and distributed maritime operations concepts.

FDCD 2017 set the design baseline for the Navy's cyber defense posture through this transition.

## Key terms from this domain {#key-terms}

Expect exam questions on this vocabulary (public-sourceable from FCC-C10F Strategic Plan and JP 3-12):

- **Tenth Fleet** — numbered fleet under FLTCYBERCOM; responsible for Navy cyberspace operations.
- **NCDOC** (Navy Cyber Defense Operations Command) — executes DODIN Operations and DCO-IDM on Navy networks.
- **NIOC** (Navy Information Operations Command) — regional cryptologic/IO commands (NIOC Georgia, NIOC Hawaii, NIOC Texas, NIOC Whidbey Island, NIOC Norfolk, NIOC Maryland, NIOC Pensacola).
- **Cyber Readiness Inspection (CRI)** — NCDOC-led assessment of Navy commands' cyber defense posture.
- **CTF (Cyber Task Force)** — operational grouping under TENTH Fleet for specific missions.
- **Afloat vs Ashore** — Navy distinguishes afloat networks (CANES on ships) from ashore (NMCI/NGEN-R at shore installations).
- **NCWDG** (Navy Cyber Warfare Development Group) — Navy's applied cyber R&D / capability-development unit.
- **FCC/C10F** — shorthand for "Fleet Cyber Command / Commander TENTH Fleet" (the dual-hat).

## Tenth Fleet C2 structure (public) {#c2}

From the FCC-C10F Strategic Plan:

- **Commander, U.S. Fleet Cyber Command / Commander, U.S. TENTH Fleet** (dual-hat, 3-star).
- Subordinate commands include NCDOC, NIOCs, and Navy Cyber Warfare Development Group (NCWDG).
- Reports ADCON to the Navy (via OPNAV N2/N6 and NAVIFOR for IW community support); OPCON to USCYBERCOM for cyber missions.

## Fleet Cyber Defense concept of operations {#conops}

FDCD describes the Navy's cyber defense approach as a **tiered model**:

1. **Host-level defense** — end-device hardening (STIGs, HBSS/ESS), EDR, patch management by the command.
2. **Enclave defense** — firewalls, IDS/IPS at each enclave boundary; ISSM-run at the local level.
3. **Theater / TENTH Fleet defense** — NCDOC monitoring of aggregate Navy traffic, correlation, hunt operations.
4. **DoD / USCYBERCOM defense** — DODIN-wide monitoring, strategic defense, joint coordination.
5. **Partner / external defense** — DHS, FBI, intel community, allied partners for threats originating outside DoD.

Each tier has its own detection, decision, and response capabilities; together they produce defense-in-depth for the Navy.

## Afloat cyber defense considerations {#afloat}

Afloat cyber defense has unique characteristics addressed in FDCD:

- **CANES afloat network** — segmented into SIPR, NIPR, SCI enclaves on a shared platform; requires specific cyber defense architecture tailored to tight-volume shipboard environments.
- **Limited reach-back** — at sea, a ship may have intermittent SATCOM; NCDOC's monitoring is degraded during comms outages.
- **Shipboard ITs and CTNs** execute local defensive actions; reach-back to NCDOC for correlation and expertise.
- **Tactical ships in contested environments** — cyber defense is integrated with EW and operational security; EMCON postures affect cyber monitoring visibility.

## Cyber Readiness Inspections (CRIs) {#cri}

The CRI is NCDOC's formal assessment mechanism for Navy commands' cyber readiness. Public CHIPS / NCDOC descriptions indicate CRIs assess:

- **Configuration compliance** (STIG posture, patch currency).
- **Vulnerability management** (ACAS scan findings, POA&M status).
- **Detection capability** (sensor coverage, log retention).
- **Incident response readiness** (trained personnel, documented procedures, exercise participation).
- **Operational integration** (user training, phishing resilience, OPSEC).

CRI findings feed into command inspection results and drive remediation priorities.

## Exam-testable concepts {#testable}

- Who commands TENTH Fleet? **Same officer as Commander, FLTCYBERCOM** (dual-hat, 3-star).
- What organization conducts Navy DCO-IDM? **NCDOC**.
- Cyber Readiness Inspection (CRI) is led by? **NCDOC**.
- What is the afloat Navy network? **CANES** (Consolidated Afloat Networks and Enterprise Services).
- What year did FDCD 2017 publish? **11 August 2017.**
- What development unit falls under FCC/C10F for applied cyber R&D? **NCWDG (Navy Cyber Warfare Development Group).**
- Name three NIOCs under FCC/C10F. **NIOC Norfolk, NIOC Hawaii, NIOC Georgia, NIOC Texas, NIOC Maryland, NIOC Pensacola, NIOC Whidbey Island — any three.**
- What TYCOM provides ADCON to FCC/C10F personnel? **NAVIFOR** (Naval Information Forces).
