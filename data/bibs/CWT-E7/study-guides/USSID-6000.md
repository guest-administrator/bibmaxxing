# USSID 6000 — US Cyber Command and Cyber Mission Force SIGINT Activities

> **🔒 Classified.** Name and number only. Adjacent public material: JP 3-12 Chapter III (USCYBERCOM authorities), and the public dual-hat structure of DIRNSA / Cdr USCYBERCOM.

**Bib study scope:** Overview; Purpose.
**Classification / Availability:** Classified. Access only on appropriate classified networks via official channels.

## What USSID 6000-series covers

USSIDs (United States Signals Intelligence Directives) in the 6000-series govern **USCYBERCOM and Cyber Mission Force SIGINT activities** — the NSA/USCYBERCOM interface for how DoD cyberspace forces use SIGINT authorities and feeds. USSID 6000 likely defines:

- The **authority framework** under which CMF teams execute SIGINT-related activities (Title 50 NSA authority vs Title 10 DoD authority).
- The **coordination procedures** between USCYBERCOM J2/J3 and NSA mission elements.
- The **reporting and dissemination** of SIGINT-derived cyber intelligence within DoD.

## The USSID directive system {#ussid-system}

USSIDs are NSA/CSS-issued directives that govern all SIGINT activities across the U.S. cryptologic system. They are the operational-policy layer between national authorities (EO 12333, FISA, NSPMs) and tactical collection:

- **USSID 18 (SP0018)** — Protection of U.S. Persons (the well-known USSID; public redacted version exists).
- **USSID 6000-series** — USCYBERCOM / CMF SIGINT activities.
- Other USSID series address: SIGINT product dissemination, foreign partner relationships, collection management, reporting formats, quality control.

Most USSIDs are classified at TS/SCI because they reference specific authorities, capabilities, or partners. USSID 18 is the exception because its core rules govern a legal framework that applies to everyone.

## Public framework for this topic {#public-framework}

You can't study USSID 6000 content, but the public doctrine around it:

- **Dual-hat command:** The Commander, USCYBERCOM is also the Director, NSA (DIRNSA). This arrangement exists to coordinate Title 10 / Title 50 authorities in cyberspace.
- **Title 50 (NSA authority):** foreign SIGINT collection, including CNE.
- **Title 10 (DoD authority):** military cyberspace operations (DCO, OCO, DODIN Ops).
- The same action (e.g., accessing a foreign C2 server) may be Title 50 (intel collection) or Title 10 (disrupt/deny) depending on the purpose, authorities invoked, and effects sought.
- **Cyber Mission Force** teams operate under Title 10 USCYBERCOM authority but may rely on Title 50 SIGINT collection for their mission preparation.

## Title 10 vs Title 50 — the central tension {#t10-t50}

The distinction matters because authorities, oversight, and legal reviews differ:

| Aspect | Title 10 (DoD) | Title 50 (NSA / IC) |
|---|---|---|
| **Statutory basis** | 10 U.S.C. (Armed Forces) | 50 U.S.C. (War and National Defense — Intelligence) |
| **Authority type** | Military operations | Foreign intelligence + CI |
| **Executing force** | CMF teams, DoD units | NSA mission elements, IC partners |
| **Oversight** | SecDef, CJCS, congressional armed services committees | DNI, NSA IG, congressional intel committees, FISC |
| **Legal review** | SJA (Staff Judge Advocate) | OGC (Office of General Counsel) |
| **Primary output** | Operational effects | Intelligence product |
| **Covert action bar** | Title 10 can be overt or covert | Covert action is Title 50 §3093 territory (Presidential Finding required) |

The dual-hat model at USCYBERCOM/NSA exists because the same infrastructure (computer networks, cyberspace) serves both authorities simultaneously, and separating them would create destructive seams in cyberspace operations.

## The Presidential Finding and covert action {#pres-finding}

When cyberspace activity crosses into **covert action** territory (an activity intended to influence foreign conditions where the U.S. role is not intended to be apparent or publicly acknowledged), 50 U.S.C. §3093 requires a **Presidential Finding**. Key features:

- Written finding signed by the President.
- Specific and reasonable in scope.
- Reported to congressional intel committees (Gang of Eight at minimum in extraordinary circumstances).
- Not tied to operational-military activity (which can be Title 10).
- Cannot violate the Constitution or specific statute.

This matters for cyber because the line between a Title 10 military cyber operation (which may be overt or classified but not "covert action") and a Title 50 covert action is a legal judgment that shapes authority paths.

## The relationship NSA ↔ USCYBERCOM {#nsa-uscc}

Beyond the dual-hat, NSA and USCYBERCOM have a formal relationship:

- **NSA Cyber Directorate (NSA/CSS)** — the NSA element focused on cyber.
- **USCYBERCOM J2** — joint intelligence directorate; relies heavily on NSA SIGINT product.
- **Cryptologic Services Groups (CSGs)** — NSA elements embedded with combatant commands.
- **Cyber National Mission Force (CNMF)** — USCYBERCOM's national mission-execution arm; draws heavily on SIGINT.

The NSA-USCC seam is managed through standing agreements, liaison personnel, and USSID 6000-series directives.

- Who is Commander, USCYBERCOM also dual-hatted as? **Director, NSA (DIRNSA).**
- Why the dual-hat? **To coordinate Title 50 (NSA SIGINT) and Title 10 (DoD cyber operations) authorities in cyberspace.**

- Who provides legal review on Title 10 cyber operations? **Staff Judge Advocate (SJA).**
- Who provides legal review on Title 50 SIGINT? **Office of General Counsel (OGC).**
- What entity oversees FISA collection? **FISC (FISA Court).**
- USSID 6000 series covers what? **USCYBERCOM / CMF SIGINT activities.**
