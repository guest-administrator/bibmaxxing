# CJCSI 3121.01B — Standing Rules of Engagement / Standing Rules for the Use of Force for US Forces

**Bib study scope:** Enclosure F.

**Source on disk:** `CJCSI-3121.01B SROE SRUF.pdf` (FOIA-released version from DoD 20-F-1436 — some original portions remain redacted but unclassified enclosures are substantively intact).

## Why this matters {#why}

The **Standing Rules of Engagement (SROE)** and **Standing Rules for the Use of Force (SRUF)** are the legal-authority baseline for every armed U.S. military action worldwide. Every Chief with operational responsibility must understand:

- What SROE vs SRUF govern.
- Inherent rights of self-defense.
- When to escalate, when to disengage.
- How ROE are amended for specific missions.

Cyber Chiefs in particular: SROE applies to cyberspace operations involving force. USCC INST 3000.14A (deconfliction) and OPTASK Cyberspace Operations ride on top of SROE authorities.

## SROE vs SRUF {#sroe-vs-sruf}

| | SROE | SRUF |
|---|---|---|
| **Applies to** | U.S. Forces operating outside U.S. territory | U.S. Forces operating in U.S. territory (land domain) or during civil support missions |
| **Legal framework** | Law of Armed Conflict (LOAC); international law | Domestic U.S. law; posse comitatus considerations |
| **Authority** | Department of Defense / commanders | DoD with civil authority interface |
| **Typical threat** | Foreign armed force, terrorist, combatant | Civilian disturbance, disaster response, border security |

For most operational deployments (Navy afloat, overseas bases, contingency ops), **SROE applies**. SRUF kicks in for domestic missions like disaster response, training, or civil-support.

## Inherent right of self-defense {#self-defense}

This is the **foundational principle** and probably the most-tested concept:

**Every commander has the inherent right and obligation to defend their unit and other U.S. forces against a hostile act or demonstrated hostile intent.**

This right:

- **Cannot be limited** by higher authority — it's inherent.
- **Can be delegated** but not delegated away.
- **Applies at all times** regardless of peacetime / wartime / operational posture.

### Levels of self-defense

- **Unit self-defense** — a commander defending their unit and adjacent units.
- **Individual self-defense** — each service member's right to defend themselves and other U.S. forces.
- **National self-defense** — defense against attack on the U.S., its forces, or other designated assets, authorized at national level.
- **Collective self-defense** — defense of designated non-U.S. forces, typically under treaty or specific authorization.

### Hostile act vs hostile intent

Critical legal distinction:

- **Hostile act** — an attack or other use of force **already occurring**.
- **Hostile intent** — threat of imminent use of force **about to occur** against the U.S., U.S. forces, or designated non-U.S. forces/persons/property.

Either justifies self-defense. **Hostile intent** gives commanders the authority to act before being struck first — "wait to be hit" is NOT doctrine.

## The principles of engagement {#principles}

Consistent with LOAC, every engagement must satisfy:

- **Necessity** — hostile act or hostile intent requires a response.
- **Proportionality** — force used must be proportionate to the threat.
- **Discrimination** — distinguish between combatants and civilians.
- **Humanity** — minimize unnecessary suffering.

## Requesting supplemental ROE {#supplemental}

Baseline SROE may be insufficient for a specific mission. Commanders can request **supplemental ROE** — additional authorities specific to their operation. Process:

1. **Commander identifies gap** — current ROE don't cover the needed action.
2. **Supplemental ROE request** — sent through chain of command to SECDEF / CJCS.
3. **Review** — legal, policy, operational evaluation.
4. **Approval** — at the authority level required (typically CJCS or SECDEF; some at POTUS).
5. **Dissemination** — down to executing forces.

## Declared Hostile Forces {#dhf}

A **Declared Hostile Force (DHF)** is a group designated by national-level authority as hostile to U.S. forces. Against a DHF:

- U.S. forces may engage based on **status** (being a member of the DHF) rather than **conduct** (hostile act / intent).
- This is a significant authority; declaration is typically at SECDEF/POTUS level.

## Cyberspace + SROE {#cyber-sroe}

SROE applies to cyberspace — but with nuances:

- **Defense of networks and systems** — inherent self-defense extends to DoD cyberspace.
- **Hostile intent in cyberspace** — may be harder to characterize than kinetic (attribution, effect prediction).
- **Offensive cyber** as force — requires national-level authority per JP 3-12 (OCO is SECDEF/POTUS approved).
- **Deconfliction** per USCC INST 3000.14A is required before cyberspace engagements.

## Reporting requirements {#reporting}

Commanders must report engagements up the chain per CJCSI 3121.01B:

- Use of force incidents — factual report of what happened.
- Casualty reports — friendly and enemy/civilian.
- Lessons learned — for future ROE refinement.

## Exam-testable concepts

- What does SROE stand for? **Standing Rules of Engagement.**
- What does SRUF stand for? **Standing Rules for the Use of Force.**
- Which applies to overseas? **SROE.** Which to domestic? **SRUF.**
- Can a commander's inherent right of self-defense be limited? **No — it's inherent.**
- Hostile act vs hostile intent — what's the difference? **Hostile act is an attack already in progress; hostile intent is a threat of imminent attack.**
- Can a commander engage based on hostile intent alone? **Yes — self-defense justifies preemptive action against imminent threat.**
- What's a DHF? **Declared Hostile Force — engagement authorized based on status, not conduct.**
- What are the four principles of engagement? **Necessity, Proportionality, Discrimination, Humanity.**
- Who approves supplemental ROE? **Typically SECDEF or CJCS (POTUS for sensitive categories).**
- Does SROE apply to cyberspace? **Yes — inherent self-defense extends to DoD networks; OCO-level force requires national authority.**
