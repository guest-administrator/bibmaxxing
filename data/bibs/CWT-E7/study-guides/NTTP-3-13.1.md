## What this document is {#what-it-is}

NTTP 3-13.1 is a **Navy Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures** publication extending **NWP 3-13 (Navy Information Operations)** to the theater and campaign planning echelons. Where NWP 3-13 is strategic doctrine, NTTP 3-13.1 translates that doctrine into planner-usable procedures: how the Navy embeds IO into theater (numbered fleet) and campaign (joint force) planning timelines, how IO annexes (Annex C, Appendix 3) are built, and how information-related capabilities (IRCs) are synchronized across the operational design.

Doctrinal hierarchy for Navy IO:

- **JP 3-13 (Information Operations)** — joint capstone doctrine.
- **NWP 3-13 (Navy Information Operations, Feb 2014)** — Navy's operational doctrine.
- **NTTP 3-13.1 (Theater/Campaign IO Planning)** — Navy's planner-level TTP at theater and campaign.
- **NTTP 3-13.2 (Navy IW Commander's Manual)** — Navy's tactical-level IW commander playbook.

The Bib tests NTTP 3-13.1 because advancement-exam questions target how a CWT Chief supports operational planning — an IW/cyber Sailor senior enough to sit in a numbered-fleet IO cell needs to know the planning vocabulary and the Annex-C structure cold.

## The Information Environment (IE) — foundational concept {#info-environment}

JP 3-13 (and NWP 3-13 by extension) defines the **Information Environment** as the aggregate of individuals, organizations, and systems that collect, process, disseminate, or act on information. The IE has three dimensions:

1. **Physical dimension** — tangible real-world hardware, people, places (printing presses, broadcast towers, server rooms, physical audiences).
2. **Informational dimension** — the actual content moving through the environment (messages, data, knowledge, perceptions).
3. **Cognitive dimension** — the minds of individuals and audiences (beliefs, values, emotional states, decision calculus). This is ultimately what IO tries to influence.

Cyberspace is one layer of the physical/informational dimensions; cyberspace operations are one IRC among several that shape the IE.

## The Information-Related Capabilities (IRCs) {#ircs}

The core IRCs recognized in joint/Navy IO doctrine:

| IRC | Purpose | Lead |
|---|---|---|
| **EW (Electronic Warfare)** | Control EM spectrum; deny/degrade enemy systems | EW officer / SIGINT |
| **MISO (Military Information Support Ops)** | Influence foreign audiences (formerly PSYOP) | Army PSYOP / MISO units |
| **MILDEC (Military Deception)** | Mislead adversary decisionmakers | Operations planners |
| **OPSEC (Operations Security)** | Protect critical information from adversary | All echelons; OPSEC officer |
| **Cyberspace Operations** | Produce effects in/through cyberspace | Service cyber components |

Supporting / closely-coordinated capabilities:

- **Public Affairs (PA)** — informs domestic and friendly audiences; must stay lane-separated from MISO.
- **Civil-Military Operations (CMO)** / Civil Affairs — engages civil populations.
- **Space Operations** — PNT, SATCOM, space-based ISR feed into IE effects.
- **Strategic Communication / Commander's Communication Synchronization (CCS)** — the coordinating mechanism above the IRCs.

## The IO planning process (theater/campaign level) {#io-planning-process}

IO integrates into the Joint Planning Process (JPP, per JP 5-0):

1. **Planning Initiation** — IO planners join the cross-functional team.
2. **Mission Analysis** — IO planners contribute to the information-environment running estimate: adversary narratives, friendly center of gravity in the IE, key audiences, critical IE vulnerabilities.
3. **COA Development** — each COA carries an IO concept identifying which IRCs produce which effects against which audience.
4. **COA Analysis and Wargaming** — red-team adversary's IE responses; wargame counter-messaging and counter-IO.
5. **COA Comparison** — weigh IO-enabled vs IO-light options.
6. **COA Approval** — commander decides; IO authorities and release authority for messaging are confirmed.
7. **Plan / Order Development** — IO Annex (Annex C, Appendix 3) is produced with objectives, tasks to IRC elements, synchronization matrix, assessment plan.

The **IO Annex** typically contains: situation (friendly + adversary IE), mission, execution (IRC tasks), coordinating instructions (deconfliction, release authority), and assessment (MOPs, MOEs).

## Objectives, tasks, and effects in IO {#objectives-tasks-effects}

Navy/joint IO planning cascades objectives top-down:

- **Commander's objective** — e.g., "Degrade adversary ability to coordinate long-range fires."
- **Information objective** — e.g., "Disrupt adversary C2 information flow across the theater."
- **IRC objective** — e.g., "Cyber: disrupt adversary C2 network X during D-hour to D+6; EW: jam adversary comms in sector Y; MILDEC: induce adversary to position fires assets incorrectly."
- **Measurable effects** — specific, observable changes in adversary behavior or capability.

Effects verbs in IO align with the cyberspace **4 Ds** (deny, degrade, disrupt, destroy), plus influence-specific verbs (influence, persuade, deter, dissuade, compel).

## Measures of Performance vs Measures of Effectiveness {#mop-moe}

- **MOP (Measure of Performance)** — did we do the thing? (Number of leaflets dropped; number of jamming minutes delivered; number of cyber missions executed.)
- **MOE (Measure of Effectiveness)** — did the thing produce the intended effect? (Did the target audience's behavior shift? Did the adversary C2 network go quiet? Did the decisionmaker make the intended decision?)

MOPs are easy to collect. MOEs are hard — they require intelligence feedback loops, indicator design, and time. Weak IO plans pile up MOPs and skip MOEs.

## Deconfliction and synchronization touchpoints {#deconfliction}

- **With Fires** — IO effects (especially cyber and EW) may conflict with kinetic targeting. A cyber effect that collects intel on a target is ruined by a cruise missile that destroys the collection platform.
- **With Intelligence** — IO consumes intelligence (targeting, audience analysis) and can damage intelligence equities (cyber operations can burn a collection stream).
- **With Public Affairs** — strict separation required. PA informs truthfully; MISO influences foreign audiences. Mixing them corrupts both.
- **With Partner Nations** — coalition IO requires standing agreements; messaging across borders needs alignment.
- **Across theaters** — an IO effect in USINDOPACOM can land in USEUCOM audiences via global media. Cross-CCMD coordination is required for regional IO campaigns.

## Cross-theater and campaign considerations {#cross-theater}

At the campaign level, IO must consider:

- **Persistent messaging** — narratives must be consistent across phases (Phase 0 shape, Phase I deter, Phase II seize, Phase III dominate, Phase IV stabilize, Phase V enable civil authority).
- **Adversary narrative contest** — what story is the adversary telling, and how do we counter it without reinforcing it?
- **Local vs strategic audience tension** — effective local messaging can produce unintended strategic backlash.
- **Legal constraints** — MISO authorities vary by theater and phase; propaganda targeting U.S. domestic audience is prohibited (Smith-Mundt).

## What's publicly known {#public-knowns}

The NWP 3-13 (2014) parent covers the same framework:

- **Five core IRCs** typically listed: Electronic Warfare (EW), Military Information Support Operations (MISO), Military Deception (MILDEC), Operations Security (OPSEC), and Cyberspace Operations. Some doctrine lists Public Affairs, Civil Affairs, and Space Operations as supporting IRCs.
- **Information Environment (IE)** is the superset; cyberspace is one layer of the IE.
- **Commander's Communication Synchronization (CCS)** process — integrates all IRCs.
- **IO cell** — staff element that plans IRCs for the commander.

JP 3-13 (you have it) is the joint authority these Navy NTTPs implement.

- What document is the parent for NTTP 3-13.1? **NWP 3-13 Navy Information Operations**.

- Name the core IRCs. **EW, MISO, MILDEC, OPSEC, Cyberspace Ops** (plus supporting: PA, Civil Affairs, Space).
- Where is the IO annex in a Joint OPLAN? **Annex C (Operations), Appendix 3 (Information Operations)**.
- At what planning levels does NTTP 3-13.1 apply? **Theater and campaign** (numbered fleet and joint force).
- Name the three dimensions of the Information Environment. **Physical, informational, cognitive.**
- What is MISO formerly known as? **PSYOP** (Psychological Operations).

- Which process above the IRCs synchronizes them? **Commander's Communication Synchronization (CCS).**
- What staff element plans IRCs for the commander? **IO cell.**
