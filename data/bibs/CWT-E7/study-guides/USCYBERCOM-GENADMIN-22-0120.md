# USCYBERCOM GENADMIN 22-0120 — SOP for Submitting a Request for Support to USCYBERCOM

> **⚠ Reference summary.** USCYBERCOM general-administrative message, 2022. CUI / controlled distribution. Public adjacent: JP 3-12 Chapter III, USCYBERCOM public fact sheets, CJCS Cyber Operations guidance.

**Bib study scope:** Section 4.
**Classification / Availability:** CUI — USCYBERCOM SharePoint / NIPR Intelink.

## What a GENADMIN is {#genadmin}

A **GENADMIN** (General Administrative) message is a standard DoD message-traffic format for admin-level instructions — SOPs, policy clarifications, procedural guidance that don't rise to the level of a formal instruction but need to be promulgated to the force quickly.

- **Not a JCS Instruction / Manual** (those are long-form doctrine).
- **Not a NAVADMIN / ALNAV / ALCOAST** (those are service-specific).
- **Is** a fast-turn policy communication tool to get procedural guidance to subordinates and supported units.

**USCYBERCOM GENADMIN 22-0120** (issued 2022) is the Standard Operating Procedure for how supported commanders, service components, and other DoD entities submit a **Request for Support (RFS)** to USCYBERCOM when they need cyberspace effects, CMF teams, or USCYBERCOM capabilities.

## What an RFS is (and isn't) {#rfs-concept}

A **Request for Support** is the formal mechanism by which a supported commander (typically a geographic combatant command, or a functional CCMD, or a service component) requests capabilities or effects from USCYBERCOM. RFS is the cyberspace analog to how other domains source capabilities:

| Domain | Request mechanism |
|---|---|
| Land forces | Request for Forces (RFF) through Joint Staff |
| Air effects | ATO nomination / JFACC tasking |
| Maritime | Fleet Operational Planning |
| **Cyberspace** | **RFS to USCYBERCOM** |
| SOF | SOF request through USSOCOM |
| Intel | RFI (Request for Information) / RFC (Request for Collection) |

RFS is **not** an RFI — an RFS asks for an action or effect, an RFI asks for information. Different channels, different approvals.

## The RFS process (public-inferable framework) {#rfs-process}

From JP 3-12 Chapter III (public) and USCYBERCOM public guidance:

1. **Requirement identified** — a supported CCMD or service component identifies a cyberspace effect or capability gap during planning (e.g., needs DCO-IDM support during a large exercise, or needs OCO effect against a specific target system as part of a combatant commander's plan).
2. **Staff coordination internal** — the requesting command's J3 / J6 / Staff Judge Advocate / intel validate that the need is real, the target is legally approvable, and the effect desired is within authority.
3. **RFS drafted** — the requester formalizes the request using USCYBERCOM's RFS format (specified fields: target, desired effect, timeline, priority, applicable authorities, deconfliction requirements, etc.).
4. **Submission** — sent through appropriate channels (typically J3-to-J3 at CCMD level, or service component to USCYBERCOM equivalent).
5. **USCYBERCOM review** — USCYBERCOM J3 and **Joint Force Headquarters–Cyberspace (JFHQ-C)** elements evaluate capability, capacity, priority relative to other tasking, legal authority, and fit with USCYBERCOM operational plans.
6. **Deconfliction** — USCC coordinates with NSA (Title 10/50 deconfliction), other agencies (ODNI, FBI where appropriate), and the Cyber Joint Operations Center for infrastructure overlap.
7. **Force allocation** — if approved, CMF teams (CPT, CMT, CST, NMT, NST) are sourced and aligned. For Navy teams, **JFHQ-Cyber Navy (C10F)** handles Navy CMF team allocation.
8. **Execution authority** — mission approval via normal USCYBERCOM chain; OCO missions may require SECDEF or POTUS approval depending on target and effect.
9. **Execution** — the approved CMF team executes, typically under a JFHQ-C operational frame.
10. **After-action reporting** — required post-mission, feeds USCYBERCOM lessons learned and future force allocation.

## JFHQ-C (Joint Force Headquarters–Cyberspace) {#jfhq-c}

USCYBERCOM established **JFHQ-Cyberspace** elements aligned to each geographic combatant command:

- **JFHQ-C Army** — supports USCENTCOM, USAFRICOM, USSOUTHCOM, USNORTHCOM.
- **JFHQ-C Navy** (FLTCYBERCOM / C10F dual-hat as JFHQ-C for PACOM/INDOPACOM alignment).
- **JFHQ-C Air Force** — supports USEUCOM.
- **JFHQ-C Marines** — limited Marine Corps support.

The JFHQ-C structure means a CCMD's RFS flows through its aligned JFHQ-C, not directly to USCYBERCOM HQ — this federates the review burden and makes Navy equities (in the case of JFHQ-C Navy) clearer in RFS processing affecting the maritime domain.

## CMF team types available via RFS {#cmf-teams}

From publicly available USCYBERCOM descriptions:

| Team type | Mission | Typical sourcing |
|---|---|---|
| **CPT (Cyber Protection Team)** | DCO-IDM — hunt and protect on specific networks | Service components provide; aligned to service needs or rotated to CCMDs |
| **CMT (Combat Mission Team)** | OCO in support of CCMD operational plans | Aligned to geographic CCMDs |
| **CST (Combat Support Team)** | Intel and analytic support to CMTs | Paired with CMTs |
| **NMT (National Mission Team)** | National-level defense against strategic adversaries | Cyber National Mission Force |
| **NST (National Support Team)** | Intel and analytic support to NMTs | Paired with NMTs |

Each team type has different authorities, different approval paths, and different deployment norms — an RFS must match the right team type to the requirement.

## Section 4 — expected content {#section-4}

Section 4 of most USCYBERCOM SOPs covers either:

- **Request format and submission mechanics** — what goes in each field of an RFS, where/how to submit, timelines, staffing cycles.
- **Prioritization criteria** — how USCYBERCOM weighs competing requests (mission criticality, CCMD priority, effect feasibility, force availability).
- **Approval authorities** — which requests need which level of approval (CCMD J3, USCYBERCOM J3, USCYBERCOM Commander, SECDEF, POTUS depending on severity).

## Legal authority framework {#authorities}

RFS processing runs into legal-authority judgments the planner must anticipate:

- **Title 10 operations** — military operations (DCO, OCO, DODIN Ops). Routine Title 10 RFS flows through standard operational chain.
- **Title 50 operations** — intelligence collection (CNE / cyberspace ISR). Typically NSA-executed but may involve USCYBERCOM infrastructure.
- **Covert action (50 U.S.C. §3093)** — requires Presidential Finding, different approval path, different oversight. A CCMD cannot request covert action via standard RFS — that is a national-level determination.
- **OCO outside declared hostilities** — requires SECDEF approval at minimum; certain effects require POTUS approval.
- **Targets on U.S. territory / U.S. persons** — heavy legal review; may require FBI coordination or DoJ review.

A well-drafted RFS addresses these authority questions up front so USCYBERCOM reviewers don't have to reverse-engineer what authority the requester is operating under.

## Navy perspective {#navy-perspective}

From a Navy (FLTCYBERCOM / C10F) perspective, GENADMIN 22-0120 governs how:

- A Fleet Commander (PACFLT, USFF, NAVEUR) submits an RFS when they need USCYBERCOM cyberspace support for a fleet operation.
- JFHQ-C Navy (C10F) reviews the RFS internally before forwarding to USCYBERCOM.
- Navy CMF teams (CMTs, CPTs assigned to Navy) get sourced against RFS tasking.
- Afloat commanders request reach-back support through NCDOC, which may feed an RFS to USCYBERCOM if the need escalates.

For a CWT E-7 this means: understand that **any cyberspace effect or CMF team support that originates outside organic Navy capability goes through the RFS process** — not via informal channels.

## Exam-testable concepts {#testable}

- What is a GENADMIN? **General Administrative message — a DoD message-traffic format for procedural guidance.**
- What does RFS stand for? **Request for Support.**
- What is the primary USCYBERCOM RFS SOP? **USCYBERCOM GENADMIN 22-0120 (2022).**
- Who adjudicates RFS priority at USCYBERCOM? **USCYBERCOM J3 / JFHQ-C elements.**
- What CMF team types can be sourced via RFS? **CPT, CMT, CST, NMT, NST.**
- What approval is needed for an RFS requesting OCO? **Ultimately SECDEF/POTUS depending on target and effect.**
- What is the difference between an RFS and an RFI? **RFS requests an action/effect; RFI requests information.**
- What is JFHQ-C Navy? **Joint Force Headquarters–Cyberspace aligned to the Navy, co-located with FLTCYBERCOM / C10F.**
- Which CCMD typically has JFHQ-C Navy support? **INDOPACOM / PACOM.**
- What legal-authority question might an RFS raise? **Title 10 vs Title 50, covert action (§3093), targets on U.S. territory.**
- Why can a CCMD not simply task Navy CMF teams directly? **CMF teams are under USCYBERCOM OPCON; tasking flows via RFS through USCYBERCOM.**
