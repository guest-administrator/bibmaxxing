## Chapter 1 — Foundations of Navy planning

Chapter 1 sets vocabulary and frames the planning environment for naval staffs.

- **Levels of warfare**: strategic, operational, tactical. Navy planning lives primarily at the operational level (Carrier Strike Group / Expeditionary Strike Group / numbered fleet) translating strategic guidance into tactical execution.
- **Mission command**: commander's intent + disciplined initiative. The published intent statement frees subordinates to adapt within the commander's left/right limits.
- **Operational art**: the application of creative thinking by commanders/staffs - using imagination - to design strategies, campaigns, and major operations and to organize and employ military forces.
- **Operational design**: the framing step that precedes detailed planning. Frame the environment, frame the problem, develop the operational approach.
- **Three time horizons** typical to a naval staff: future plans (J5/N5), future operations (J35/N35), current operations (J3/N3). NWP 5-01 product flows feed each.

Key Chapter 1 concepts the Bib tests cold:

- The difference between *commander's intent*, *guidance*, and *direction*.
- Why operational design comes before COA development (you frame the problem before you propose solutions).
- The role of *assumptions* and *constraints/restraints* in framing.
- The output of Chapter 1's framing block is the **mission analysis** input - it bridges into Chapter 2.

## Chapter 2 — The Navy Planning Process (NPP)

Chapter 2 is the procedural core. NWP 5-01 codifies a **six-step NPP** that mirrors and extends the joint planning steps from JP 5-0:

1. **Mission Analysis**
   - Receive higher HQ tasking.
   - Identify specified, implied, and essential tasks.
   - Produce the **restated mission**, **commander's intent**, **planning guidance**, **CCIRs**.
   - **CCIRs** = Commander's Critical Information Requirements = **PIRs** (Priority Intelligence Requirements) + **FFIRs** (Friendly Force Information Requirements).
2. **Course of Action (COA) Development**
   - Generate distinct COAs that are **suitable, feasible, acceptable, distinguishable, and complete** (SFADC).
3. **COA Analysis (Wargaming)**
   - Run each COA against enemy COAs in a wargame.
   - Record outputs: **decision points**, **branches**, **sequels**, **risks**, **commander's decisions**.
4. **COA Comparison**
   - Apply weighted evaluation criteria; identify the best COA per criterion.
5. **COA Approval / Decision**
   - Commander selects a COA (or directs revisions). The decision brief is a named, scheduled product.
6. **Plan or Order Development**
   - Produce the **OPLAN / OPORD / FRAGORD** with all annexes (intel, ops, logistics, cyber, info-ops, etc.).

The NPP outputs feed the joint **APEX** (Adaptive Planning and Execution) system that JP 5-0 governs at the joint level.

### Staff organization for planning

The planning staff is typically organized as an **Operations Planning Team (OPT)** built around N3 and N5 leadership with subject-matter expertise pulled from every staff code:

- N3 - current and future operations
- N5 - future plans (where NWP 5-01 lives day-to-day)
- N2 - intelligence
- N4 - logistics
- N6 - communications / cyber
- N7/N9 - training, exercises, fleet integration

The OPT is convened, briefed, runs the steps above, and is dissolved when the order is published.

### Output formats

- **OPLAN / OPORD** uses the standard **five-paragraph format**:
  1. Situation
  2. Mission
  3. Execution
  4. Sustainment (logistics)
  5. Command and Signal
- **FRAGORD** = fragmentary order; modifies an existing OPORD without restating the whole thing.

## Appendix C — (Operational Design / Detailed Planning Considerations)

The Bib calls out Appendix C specifically for the Substitute exam. Across editions, NWP 5-01's appendices cover:

- Operational design products (problem framing, operational approach).
- Detailed planning considerations (OPORD format, annex contents, cross-walks to joint annexes).
- Sample planning products and brief templates.

**Verify the current Appendix C** against the May-2021 / April-2025 NWC PDF in your reading; appendices shift as revisions arrive. Two markers to confirm on first read:

- The Appendix C operational-design vocabulary (centers of gravity, critical capabilities, critical requirements, critical vulnerabilities) should match JP 5-0's COG analysis.
- The Appendix C product templates should reference the same five-paragraph OPORD structure described in Chapter 2.

## Common exam-style question stems (open-source framing)

- "Which document is the Navy operationalization of JP 5-0?" -> **NWP 5-01, Navy Planning**.
- "How many steps in the Navy Planning Process?" -> **Six** (Mission Analysis, COA Development, COA Analysis, COA Comparison, COA Approval/Decision, Plan/Order Development).
- "Which NPP step is the wargame?" -> **Step 3: COA Analysis**.
- "What test do COAs need to pass during COA Development?" -> Suitable, Feasible, Acceptable, Distinguishable, Complete (SFADC).
- "CCIRs are composed of which two element types?" -> **PIRs and FFIRs**.
- "Which staff codes lead the OPT?" -> **N3 and N5** (with SME augmentation from N2/N4/N6/N7).
- "Standard OPORD format?" -> **Five paragraphs**: Situation / Mission / Execution / Sustainment / Command and Signal.
- "Difference between OPLAN, OPORD, and FRAGORD?" -> OPLAN is a planned future order; OPORD is the current executed order; FRAGORD modifies an OPORD without restating it.

## How to actually study this on the boat

1. Download the public NWC PDF (link at the top). Read **Chapter 1** cover to cover - operational design vocabulary tracks JP 5-0 and ADP 5-0.
2. Read **Chapter 2** and outline the six NPP steps with input/output products. Most testable concepts live here.
3. Read **Appendix C** in full; cross-walk the operational-design language against JP 5-0 Ch 4.
4. If you've planned a real ship or staff event, do the doctrine-to-practice cross-walk: "the OPORD we wrote for X used annex Y for cyber - which NPP step produced annex Y?"
5. Tie back: every NPP step has a joint-planning counterpart in JP 5-0. Knowing the joint name (mission analysis is mission analysis at both levels) lets you cross-reference fast under exam pressure.
