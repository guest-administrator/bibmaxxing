# JP 5-0 — Joint Planning

**Bib study scope:** Chapters I, II, IV; Executive Summary.

**Source on disk:** `JP-5-0 Joint Planning.pdf`.

## Why this publication matters {#why}

Joint Planning is the **process and doctrine** by which combatant commanders and joint task force commanders turn strategic guidance into executable military plans. Every Chief must understand how operations get planned because cyberspace effects are integrated into joint plans through this process. JP 5-0 is heavily referenced in JP 3-12 Chapter IV (which you've already studied).

## Strategic context (Chapter I) {#chapter-i}

### The strategic environment

Joint planners work with four recurring concepts:

- **Ends** — what the commander wants to achieve (the strategic objective / desired end state).
- **Ways** — how (the strategy and operational approach).
- **Means** — what resources will be used (forces, logistics, authorities, time).
- **Risks** — what could go wrong and what's acceptable.

### National strategic direction

Operations are directed by a cascade of national documents:

- **National Security Strategy (NSS)** — President's strategic guidance.
- **National Defense Strategy (NDS)** — SECDEF's DoD-wide priorities.
- **National Military Strategy (NMS)** — CJCS's military priorities.
- **Unified Command Plan (UCP)** — assigns CCMDs their AORs and missions.
- **Guidance for Employment of the Force (GEF)** and **Joint Strategic Campaign Plan (JSCP)** — SECDEF direction to CCMDs.

Chief-level insight: cyberspace operations are integrated into CCMD campaign plans through this chain, with USCYBERCOM supporting as described in JP 3-12 Ch III.

### Plan types

Joint plans are categorized:

- **Campaign plans (CAMPLANs)** — enduring, strategic-level plans for an AOR.
- **Contingency plans (CONPLANs / OPLANs)** — for specific contingencies (war plans).
- **Operation orders (OPORDs)** — for ongoing or near-term operations.
- **Fragmentary orders (FRAGORDs)** — amendments to OPORDs.

### The campaigning construct (updated)

Modern joint doctrine emphasizes **integrated campaigning** across the competition continuum: cooperation, competition below armed conflict, and armed conflict. Cyberspace operations contribute in all three.

## Joint Planning Process (JPP) — Chapter IV {#chapter-iv}

The JPP is JP 5-0's heart. **7 steps — memorize in order:**

1. **Planning Initiation** — receipt of strategic direction; planning team stood up.
2. **Mission Analysis** — what are we being asked to do?
3. **COA Development** — build several candidate courses of action.
4. **COA Analysis and Wargaming** — test each COA against likely adversary reactions.
5. **COA Comparison** — evaluate COAs against each other using weighted criteria.
6. **COA Approval** — commander picks one.
7. **Plan or Order Development** — turn the chosen COA into a written plan.

### Step 1 — Planning Initiation

Trigger events: strategic direction, anticipated mission, crisis.

Outputs: planning team, initial planning timeline, warning order (WARNORD) to subordinates.

### Step 2 — Mission Analysis (MA)

Most important step; drives everything after. Produces:

- **Restated mission** — what we're doing, in a format that answers who/what/when/where/why.
- **Commander's intent** (draft) — purpose, method, end state.
- **Specified, implied, essential tasks** — parsed from the mission statement.
- **Constraints and restraints** — must-do and must-not-do boundaries.
- **Assumptions** — things we take as given for planning, to be revisited.
- **Critical facts** — verified conditions.
- **Critical CCIRs (Commander's Critical Information Requirements)** — info the commander must have.
- **Initial IPB (Intelligence Preparation of the Battlespace/Operational Environment)** — from J2.
- **Mission Analysis Brief** to the commander.

### Step 3 — COA Development

Build **multiple COAs** (typically 2-4). Each COA must be:

- **Suitable** — accomplishes the mission.
- **Feasible** — can be done with available resources.
- **Acceptable** — cost and risk are proportionate.
- **Distinguishable** — meaningfully different from other COAs (not trivial variations).
- **Complete** — answers who, what, when, where, why, how.

### Step 4 — COA Analysis and Wargaming

Test each COA against likely enemy actions and friendly responses. Methods:

- **Action-reaction-counteraction** — step through each phase and consider adversary responses.
- **Synchronization matrix** — align friendly actions across time and warfighting function.
- **Decision-support template (DST)** — ties COA decisions to conditions / triggers.

Outputs: refined COAs, identified risks, decision points, branches, sequels.

### Step 5 — COA Comparison

Rank COAs using weighted criteria (e.g., risk, speed, casualties, political sensitivity, logistics). Recommend one to the commander.

### Step 6 — COA Approval

Commander decides. Can choose one COA, blend elements, or direct replanning.

### Step 7 — Plan/Order Development

Transform the chosen COA into a written plan with standard format:

- **Five-paragraph format**: Situation, Mission, Execution, Sustainment, Command & Signal.
- **Annexes A-Z**: Task Organization, Intelligence, Operations (with cyberspace Appendix 3), Logistics, etc.
- **Tabs / Appendices**: Detailed supporting content.

## Operational design (Chapter II) {#chapter-ii}

Operational design is the **conceptual framework** that precedes detailed planning. It helps commanders and planners understand the problem before prescribing a solution.

### Elements of operational design

- **Termination** — what conditions must exist to end operations?
- **End state and objectives** — what's the desired end state?
- **Centers of gravity (COG)** — sources of power that provide strength, freedom of action, or will to act.
  - **Friendly COG** — our critical source of strength (protect it).
  - **Adversary COG** — theirs (attack it).
  - **Critical capabilities, requirements, and vulnerabilities** — COGs are analyzed through capabilities needed to perform their function, requirements to have those capabilities, and vulnerabilities that expose those requirements.
- **Decisive points** — specific geographic, time, or virtual locations where a marked advantage can be gained.
- **Lines of operation (LOOs)** and **lines of effort (LOEs)** — how activities connect causes to effects over time.
- **Phases** — temporal divisions of an operation (0 Shape, I Deter, II Seize Initiative, III Dominate, IV Stabilize, V Enable Civil Authority).
- **Culminating point** — the point at which an operation can no longer continue.

### Relationships between design + JPP

Operational design **informs** the JPP; it's not a separate process. Mission analysis incorporates initial design elements; COA development refines them.

## Integrated campaigning {#integrated-campaigning}

Modern JP 5-0 emphasizes that planning spans:

- **Cooperation** — with allies, partners, civil authorities.
- **Competition below armed conflict** — "gray zone" activities (information ops, cyber harassment, economic coercion, proxy support).
- **Armed conflict** — traditional warfighting.

Cyberspace operations are a primary instrument in competition-below-armed-conflict. JP 5-0's planning framework applies to all three.

## Assessment {#assessment}

Plans aren't done when approved — assessment is continuous:

- **Measures of Performance (MOP)** — did we do what we planned? (activities/outputs)
- **Measures of Effectiveness (MOE)** — did our actions produce the desired effect? (outcomes)
- **Indicators** — observable data that informs MOPs and MOEs.
- **Assessment cycle** — monitor → evaluate → direct — continuous reassessment.

## Integration with cyberspace operations (from JP 3-12 Ch IV) {#cyber-integration}

Cyberspace planners integrate into the joint planning team from Step 1 onward. Cyber-specific considerations at each step:

- **Mission Analysis** — cyber-relevant tasks, authorities, intelligence gain/loss.
- **COA Development** — cyber options, dependencies, deconfliction requirements.
- **COA Analysis** — adversary cyber capabilities, collateral effects, attribution risk.
- **Plan Development** — cyberspace appendix (Annex C, Appendix 3), cyber CONOPS.

## Exam-testable concepts

- How many steps in the JPP? **7.**
- Name step 2. **Mission Analysis.**
- What tests applied to a COA? **Suitable, Feasible, Acceptable, Distinguishable, Complete.**
- Five-paragraph order format? **Situation, Mission, Execution, Sustainment, Command & Signal.**
- What is a Center of Gravity? **A source of power that provides strength, freedom of action, or will to act — friendly or adversary.**
- MOP vs MOE? **MOP = are we doing what we planned? MOE = is the effect occurring?**
- Phases I–V of a traditional joint operation? **Shape, Deter, Seize Initiative, Dominate, Stabilize, Enable Civil Authority** (with Phase 0 Shape preceding).
- Document that assigns CCMDs their AORs? **UCP — Unified Command Plan.**
- Where does cyberspace planning live in an OPLAN? **Annex C, Appendix 3** (typically).
