# JP 3-0 — Joint Campaigns and Operations

**Bib study scope:** Chapter VII.

**Source on disk:** `JP-3-0 Joint Campaigns and Operations.pdf`.

## Why this publication matters {#why}

JP 3-0 is the **capstone operations doctrine** — every other 3-series joint pub is subordinate to it. It defines how joint forces campaign and conduct operations across the competition continuum. Chief-level mastery is expected.

Chapter VII typically covers **joint functions** — the overarching framework through which joint forces organize and apply military power.

## The seven joint functions (Chapter VII core content) {#joint-functions}

The joint functions are the framework for thinking about what a joint force does. **Memorize the seven:**

1. **Command and Control (C2)** — authority and direction to forces; includes the commander, staff, procedures, and systems.
2. **Intelligence** — understanding of the operational environment, adversary, and friendly forces.
3. **Fires** — use of weapon systems or lethal/non-lethal effects to create desired outcomes.
4. **Movement and Maneuver** — disposition and motion of forces to achieve positional advantage.
5. **Protection** — preservation of friendly forces, installations, operations, and capabilities.
6. **Sustainment** — logistics, personnel services, and health services to enable endurance and operational reach.
7. **Information** — management and application of information to affect the understanding and decisions of audiences.

**Information** was formally added as the seventh joint function in 2017 (JP 3-0 Change 1); older references may omit it.

## How cyberspace operations contribute to each function {#cyber-contrib}

This is a high-frequency exam topic:

| Joint function | How cyber contributes |
|---|---|
| **C2** | DODIN operations provide the secure networks on which C2 rides |
| **Intelligence** | Cyberspace ISR + fused feeds into all-source analysis |
| **Fires** | OCO creates denial/degradation/disruption/destruction effects |
| **Movement and Maneuver** | Cyber enables freedom of maneuver in cyberspace; denies it to adversaries |
| **Protection** | DCO and DODIN Ops protect friendly systems |
| **Sustainment** | Cyber supports logistics systems, personnel records, medical data |
| **Information** | Cyberspace is a principal medium for IO effects |

Exam note: a question asking "which joint function does cyberspace support?" usually has **all of the above / all seven** as the correct answer.

## Unified action (public doctrine) {#unified-action}

**Unified action** is the synchronization, coordination, and/or integration of the activities of governmental and nongovernmental entities with military operations to achieve unity of effort.

Key relationships:

- **Joint force** — two or more U.S. military services integrated under a single commander.
- **Multinational force** — multiple nations contributing forces.
- **Interagency** — U.S. governmental agencies beyond DoD (State, DHS, FBI, USAID, CIA).
- **NGO / private sector** — unity of effort rather than command.

**Unity of effort vs unity of command** — Joint forces operate under unity of command (one commander); in interagency / multinational contexts, the goal is unity of effort (shared purpose, coordinated action, no single commander over all).

## Principles of joint operations {#principles}

JP 3-0 inherits the classic **principles of war** plus modern additions. You should recognize:

### Principles of war (9)

1. **Objective** — direct every military operation toward a clearly defined, decisive, attainable goal.
2. **Offensive** — seize, retain, exploit the initiative.
3. **Mass** — concentrate combat power at the decisive place and time.
4. **Economy of Force** — allocate minimum essential force to secondary efforts.
5. **Maneuver** — place the enemy in a position of disadvantage through flexible force application.
6. **Unity of Command** — one responsible commander for every objective.
7. **Security** — prevent the enemy from acquiring unexpected advantage.
8. **Surprise** — strike at a time, place, or manner for which the enemy is unprepared.
9. **Simplicity** — prepare clear, uncomplicated plans and orders.

### Additional joint principles

- **Restraint** — prudent application of force per rules of engagement.
- **Perseverance** — prepared for protracted operations; measured persistent effort.
- **Legitimacy** — domestic and international law, mandates, popular support.

## The operational environment (OE) {#oe}

The OE is the **composite of conditions, circumstances, and influences** that affect the employment of capabilities and bear on the decisions of the commander. Analyzed via:

- **PMESII-PT** — Political, Military, Economic, Social, Infrastructure, Information, Physical environment, Time. Framework for understanding the OE.
- **METT-TC** — Mission, Enemy, Terrain and weather, Troops and support available, Time available, Civil considerations. Framework for mission analysis.

## Conflict continuum (modern JP 3-0) {#continuum}

Modern joint doctrine frames operations across three conditions:

- **Cooperation** — building partnerships, combined exercises, security assistance.
- **Competition below armed conflict** — adversarial pressure without open hostilities (information ops, cyber harassment, economic coercion, proxy activity).
- **Armed conflict** — use of force consistent with ROE and international law.

Joint forces operate across all three simultaneously, not just during declared wars.

## Instruments of national power (DIME) {#dime}

Traditional instruments:

- **D**iplomatic
- **I**nformational
- **M**ilitary
- **E**conomic

Military power is ONE of four; cyberspace operations can bridge M and I. Some modern treatments use **DIMEFIL** (adding Financial, Intelligence, Law enforcement) for whole-of-government analysis.

## Joint force employment {#employment}

### Phasing

Operations are conceptually phased (not necessarily linear or sequential):

- **Phase 0 — Shape** — activities to influence the environment and prevent conflict.
- **Phase I — Deter** — activities to deter undesired adversary action.
- **Phase II — Seize Initiative** — early operations to gain advantage.
- **Phase III — Dominate** — main combat operations.
- **Phase IV — Stabilize** — post-combat stabilization.
- **Phase V — Enable Civil Authority** — transition to civil control.

### Command relationships

| Relationship | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **COCOM** (Combatant Command) | Non-transferable authority of CCDR |
| **OPCON** (Operational Control) | Assigning subordinate forces and directing operations |
| **TACON** (Tactical Control) | Control over movements and maneuvers for a specific mission |
| **ADCON** (Administrative Control) | Authority over subordinate units for admin / support (non-operational) |
| **Supporting/supported** | One commander aids another (as in USCYBERCOM ↔ geographic CCMD) |

## Centers of Gravity (COG) analysis {#cog}

Operational design uses **Center of Gravity** analysis to focus effects:

- **Center of Gravity** — the source of power (physical or moral) that provides freedom of action, strength, or will to act.
- **Critical Capabilities (CC)** — primary abilities considered crucial to the COG's function.
- **Critical Requirements (CR)** — conditions, resources, and means essential for the CC to be fully operational.
- **Critical Vulnerabilities (CV)** — CRs that are deficient or vulnerable to neutralization, interdiction, or attack.

Planners target CVs to degrade CCs to weaken the COG — this is the planning logic behind operational design.

## Operational reach and tempo {#reach-tempo}

Two planning concepts JP 3-0 emphasizes:

- **Operational reach** — the distance and duration across which a force can successfully employ military capabilities. Constrained by sustainment, communications, ISR coverage.
- **Tempo** — the rate of military action. Joint forces aim for tempo that exceeds adversary decision-making speed — the "OODA loop" concept (Observe, Orient, Decide, Act).

Cyber operations affect both:
- Cyber extends operational reach (can affect adversary thousands of miles away without forward-deploying platforms).
- Cyber accelerates tempo (automated effects can execute faster than human decision cycles — raising AI/automation issues).

## Lines of Operation and Lines of Effort {#loo-loe}

Joint operations organize along lines:

- **Line of Operation (LOO)** — physical or logical orientation in time and space connecting actions to objectives. Often geographic.
- **Line of Effort (LOE)** — logical linkage of multiple tasks/effects toward establishing conditions. Not geographic.

Modern joint operations — especially competition-phase ops — use LOEs because effects are logical, not geographic (e.g., "build partner capacity" or "counter adversary malign influence").

## Mission command {#mission-command}

**Mission command** is the philosophy for how commanders lead:

- Empower subordinates with commander's intent.
- Decentralized execution.
- Trust.
- Disciplined initiative.
- Acceptance of prudent risk.

Applies to cyber ops — where tactical decision windows can be seconds, and the commander's intent must cover actions too fast for approval loops.

## Exam-testable concepts

- How many joint functions? **7** (post-2017).
- Name the joint function added in 2017? **Information.**
- DIME stands for? **Diplomatic, Informational, Military, Economic.**
- Phase 0 is called? **Shape.**
- Phase III is called? **Dominate.**
- What's the difference between OPCON and TACON? **OPCON directs operations; TACON is narrower — just tactical movements and maneuvers for a specific mission.**
- Which joint function does DCO primarily support? **Protection.**
- What does METT-TC analyze? **Mission, Enemy, Terrain/weather, Troops available, Time available, Civil considerations.**
- Unity of effort vs unity of command? **Unity of command = one boss in military hierarchy; unity of effort = shared purpose/coordination without single hierarchy (interagency, multinational).**
- What is a Center of Gravity? **The source of power (physical or moral) that provides freedom of action, strength, or will to act.**
- What's the difference between LOO and LOE? **LOO is physical/geographic; LOE is logical/conceptual — a set of tasks linked to conditions.**
- What's the OODA loop? **Observe, Orient, Decide, Act — the decision-making cycle tempo target.**
- What philosophy empowers decentralized subordinate execution? **Mission command.**
- What three conditions frame the conflict continuum? **Cooperation, Competition below armed conflict, Armed conflict.**
- What U.S. authority defines COCOM? **10 U.S.C. §164 — authority of the CCDR over assigned forces.**
